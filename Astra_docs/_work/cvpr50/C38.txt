Abstract
While recent foundation models for remote sensing segmen-
tation have shown notable progress, they still fall short in
processing diverse multi-modal inputs, synergizing comple-
mentary prompt types, and leveraging semantic hierarchies.
To address these limitations, we introduce SkySense-VITA,
a unified in-context segmentation model, which synergisti-
cally processes both optical and Synthetic Aperture Radar
(SAR) imagery usingVIsual,TextuAl, or fused prompts.
Based on a novel prompt-and-prediction decoupling strat-
egy, we propose the VITA-Former and VITA-Decoder to de-
couple multi-modal prompt fusion and prediction process,
allowing the model to flexibly support visual-only, textual-
only, and fused prompt modes. We train SkySense-VITA with
a progressive two-stage strategy: a first stage of Image-
Level Alignment Pretraining featuring optical-SAR align-
ment, and a second stage of Pixel-Level In-context Pretrain-
ing using Semantic Granularity Annealing (SGA), a coarse-
to-fine curriculum that enables robust hierarchical learn-
ing. To support this training, we introduce our new large-
scale, multi-modal Sky-VT-300k dataset. Extensive experi-
ments show SkySense-VITA establishes a new state-of-the-
art (SOTA) on 18 datasets, with an average performance
lead of over 10% mean Intersection over Union (mIoU).

damental technology for Earth observation, critical for ap-
plications like urban planning and disaster response [2,
47, 52]. However, the dominant “one model, one task”
paradigm relies on specialized models trained with expen-
sive, large-scale annotated datasets. This dependency re-
sults in poor generalization to new geographic regions, sen-
sor types, or classes, hindering scalable planetary analysis.

from a critical limitation: their adaptation requires a time-
consuming and labor-intensive fine-tuning process, which
is impractical for time-critical applications. More recently,
a new frontier has emerged with fine-tuning-free universal
segmentation models. A representative work is the Segment
Anything Model (SAM) [14, 35] shown in Fig. 1(b), which
achieves rapid task adaptation via per-image user interac-
tion without parameter modification. However, SAM is lim-
ited for remote sensing, as it is trained on natural images and
struggles with the domain gap, especially for SAR images.
Furthermore, its reliance on per-image user interaction pre-
vents large-scale automated processing. While pioneering
works [17, 52, 56, 60] have explored prompt-based archi-
tectures for universal segmentation in remote sensing, they
suffer from three key limitations:
First, limited modality support. Remote sensing imagery
is inherently multi-modal, with optical sensors providing
rich textural details and SAR offering all-weather, all-time
capability [9]. A unified segmentation model should sup-
port both modalities for robust deployment, while current
methods [17, 60] are designed exclusively for optical im-
agery, rendering them inapplicable to SAR imagery and
limiting real-world application.
Second, limited prompt type support. Existing universal
models in remote sensing are divided into visual-prompt-
based [52] and textual-prompt-based methods [17, 56, 60],
each with distinct limitations. Visual prompts, which use
reference images and masks, provide rich visual context but
suffer from task granularity ambiguity. For instance, acar
annotation could also mean thevehicle, or thetransporta-
tionsuper-category. Textual-prompt-based methods suffer
from semantic ambiguity. For instance, a single term like
plantcan refer to eithervegetationor apower plant. They
also fail to capture vast intra-class visual variance, as text
alone cannot encode specific stylistic nuances, such as the
dramatically different appearance ofhousesacross conti-
nents [43]. Consequently, jointly supporting both prompt
types is crucial for flexible and robust generalization.
Third, limited semantic hierarchy consideration. Remote
sensing categories are naturally hierarchical (e.g.,car⊂ve-
hicle). Existing methods [17, 52, 56] typically treat classes
as a flat list, ignoring crucial structural knowledge and forc-
ing the model to learn difficult, fine-grained classes from
scratch without the benefit of coarse-level contextual pri-
ors. It also exacerbates inter-class confusion, thereby com-
promising the structure and robustness of the feature space.
To address these limitations, we introduce SkySense-
VITA, a unified in-context segmentation model that pro-
cesses both optical and SAR imagery and supports visual,
textual, and fused prompts, where “in-context” means seg-
mentation conditioned on external prompts (visual exem-
plars, textual descriptors, or their combinations) without
task-specific fine-tuning. To train model at scale, we con-
struct Sky-VT-300k, a large dataset of optical and SAR re-
mote sensing images with pixel-level semantic annotations
and aligned visual exemplars and textual descriptors, cov-
ering more than 300,000 samples across 176 categories.
Building on SkySense’s decoupled backbone [9], we in-
troduce learnable modality tokens that aggregate modality-
specific features across layers, improving robustness un-
der heterogeneous sensors. We further develop VITA-
Former and VITA-Decoder based on a decoupling strat-
egy for prompts and predictions: VITA-Former performs
prompt decoupling, fusing visual and textual cues while
retaining modality-specific pathways; VITA-Decoder per-
forms prediction decoupling, jointly optimizing the visual,
textual, and fused branches to mitigate cross-branch inter-
ference and maximize per-branch performance. Finally, we
introduce a progressive two-stage pretraining pipeline: 1)
Image-Level Alignment Pretraining, which aligns optical
and SAR representations via cross-modal learning; and 2)
Pixel-Level In-Context Pretraining with Semantic Granular-
ity Annealing (SGA), a semantic-tree–guided coarse-to-fine
curriculum that reduces semantic conflicts and improves
generalization. Experiments on in-distribution and out-of-
distribution benchmarks show that SkySense-VITA consis-
tently outperforms prior models across datasets and metrics.
Our main contributions are:
• We introduce SkySense-VITA, a unified in-context seg-
mentation model that jointly supports optical/SAR inputs
and visual, textual, and fused prompting.
• We develop a progressive pretraining strategy named
SGA to obtain robust, hierarchy-aware representations.
• Extensive evaluations on 18 datasets demonstrate SOTA
performance, achieving an average gain exceeding 10%
mIoU compared to existing baselines.
2. Related Work
2.1. Remote Sensing Foundation Model
RSFMs excel at learning robust feature representations
from extensive unlabeled data via self-supervised learning.
Predominant methods include contrastive learning [9, 12,
24, 26, 50] and Masked Image Modeling [6, 18, 21, 31, 39],
with some works combing both [27, 29]. Despite excel-
lent generalization on diverse data, these RSFMs necessi-
tate task-specific fine-tuning for downstream segmentation
tasks. To overcome it, we propose SkySense-VITA, an
in-context segmentation model that handles diverse remote
sensing tasks without fine-tuning.
2.2. In-context Segmentation
In-context learning was popularized by GPT-3 [1] and
adapted to vision by Painter [48]. SegGPT [49] fur-
ther modeled in-context segmentation via in-context color-
ing, enabling few-shot generalization without fine-tuning.

Subsequent work falls into three paradigms: (i) SAM-
based [23, 38, 40, 57], which generate prompts via cross-
image correspondence via DINOv2 [32] and segment with

cross-image correspondences. In remote sensing, Sky-
Sense++ [52] introduces semantic-band to transfer in-
context learning to randomized index segmentation, im-

2.3. Open-V ocabulary Segmentation for Remote
Sensing
Open-vocabulary semantic segmentation aims to segment
arbitrary categories in the open world by textual prompts.
In remote sensing, SegEarth-OV [17] is the first training-
free open-vocabulary semantic segmentation framework,
introducing a feature upsampler to recover spatial detail.
SkySense-O [60] further adopts a vision-centric modeling
principle, leveraging knowledge graph to correct alignment
ambiguities induced by language guidance.
Most above methods support only one prompt type and
therefore inherit its limitations. Although some [13, 36]
combine visual and textual prompts, they operate on per-
image prompts in natural images and lack mechanisms to
synergize both. In contrast, SkySense-VITA tackles the un-
derexplored cross-image in-context setting, supporting vi-
sual, textual, and fused prompts across optical and SAR via
prompt–prediction decoupling for robust fusion.
3. SkySense-VITA
In this section, we present the pretraining dataset, model
architecture and pretraining details of SkySense-VITA.
3.1. Pretraining Dataset
For Image-level Alignment Pretraining, we create a dataset
of co-located optical-SAR-text triplets by adding SAR im-
agery with the optical imagery from [60]. For pixel-level
in-context pretraining, we construct Sky-VT-300k dataset, a
diverse dataset of over 300,000 samples aggregated from 12
sources [8, 20, 30, 41, 44–46, 51, 52, 54, 60]. This dataset
spans 7 satellite platforms and 176 classes, covers both op-
tical and SAR modalities, and provides pixel-level masks
with aligned visual exemplars and textual descriptors for in-
context training.
3.2. Model Architecture
In Fig. 2, our architecture has two flows: a target flow en-
codes the target image and generates segmentation masks,
and a prompt flow encodes visual and/or textual prompts to
produce in-context queries. The overall pipeline consists of
three parts: Multi-modal Feature Extraction, Multi-modal
Prompt Fusion, and Prompt-Guided Decoding.
Multi-modal Feature Extraction.Given a target image
xtgt and a prompt pair(x pmt, vpmt, tpmt), wherex pmt is
the prompt image,v pmt is its visual annotation, andt pmt
is the class name. We extract featuresF tgt andF pmt us-
ing a factorized encoder [9] from optical and SAR inputs.
We construct visual prompt tokensP V ∈R N×C by class-
wise masked average pooling fromF pmt andv pmt. Textual
prompt tokensP T ∈R N×C are obtained by encoding cate-
gory namest pmt with a pretrained text encoder [33].

tual, and fused prompt tokens (Fig. 3). We first mitigate
the optical/SAR discrepancy by injecting modality proto-
typesP M into the visual tokens via cross-attention, yield-

3.3. Pretraining
Our pretraining employs a progressive two-stage pipeline.
The first stage focuses on Image-level Alignment Pretrain-
ing to bridge the domain gap between the optical and SAR.
The second stage, Pixel-level In-context Pretraining, learns
to segment prompt-specified categories under a semantic
hierarchy, enabling coarse-to-fine learning with visual and
textual prompts.

4. Experiments
4.1. Implementation Details
Pretraining.The first Image-level Alignment Pretraining
runs on a cluster of 80 A100 GPUs. The second Pixel-
Level In-context Pretraining runs on 8 A100 GPUs. For this
stage, we train the model on our new Sky-VT-300k dataset
for 10,000 iterations with a batch size of 64. More details,
including optimizer settings and learning rate schedules, are
included in the supplementary materials.
Architecture.We employ the factorized SkySense encoder
from [9] and the pretrained text encoder from CLIP [33].
The total model size is 1.1B parameters. This is composed
of 969M parameters for the image encoder and 149M for
the text encoder. The VITA-Former and VITA-Decoder are
lightweight and efficient, comprising only 18M parameters.
4.2. Performance on In-distribution Tasks
We adopt FLAIR [8], Potsdam [10], FBP [44],
LoveDA [46], iSAID [51], JL-16 [52], OAM-TCD [45],
GF1-WHU [20], AIR-PV [54], QuickQuake Buildings [41],
and ETCI-Flood [30] datasets for in-distribution evaluation.
The pretraining datasets and evaluation benchmarks are
completely disjoint to avoid data leakage. We report mIoU
of in-context segmentation models under one-shot setting
and open-vocabulary segmentation models under zero-shot
setting. For our SkySense-VITA, we apply these one-shot
and zero-shot settings to its visual-only and textual-only
modes, respectively.

4.3. Performance on Out-of-distribution Tasks
We evaluate out-of-distribution generalization from two
critical perspectives. First, we assess domain generalization
on [5, 7, 11, 19], where test datasets do not overlap with
the training data, but their categories are seen during train-
ing. For example, the Vaihingen [11] shares classes with the
Potsdam [10] but differs significantly in domain (e.g., sen-
sor characteristics, geographic location). This setup strin-
gently evaluates a model’s ability to generalize to new do-
mains for known categories. Second, we assess class gen-
eralization on [34, 58, 59]. In these tasks, both the domain
and the majority of classes are novel (e.g.,flooded roadis
not present in the training set). This evaluation measures
the model’s generalization capability across categories.
Table 2 presents the results for both domain and class
generalization. In the domain generalization evaluation,
generalist models trained on natural images continue to ex-
hibit limited performance. The in-context segmentation
methods, benefiting from the inclusion of domain-specific
visual information , achieve better results. For instance,
SkySense++ leads the best-performing open vocabulary
model, SkySense-O, by 49.88% on GLH-Water dataset,
highlighting the advantage of in-context visual prompts
for domain adaptation. Our SkySense-VITA demonstrates
competitive performance under the visual-prompt setting,
and under textual and fused prompts consistently outper-
forms all other methods, demonstrating superior domain
generalization ability. In the class generalization tasks,
SkySense-VITA demonstrates an outstanding capability for
discovering new classes, which we attribute to its synergis-
tic support for both visual and textual prompts. This is par-
ticularly evident on the FloodNet dataset, where the fused
prompt mode achieves an mIoU of 62.19 %, surpassing the
best single-modality prompt, textual-only, by over 4%.
We provide a qualitative comparison by visualizing the
outputs of various models on the GLH-Water datasets in
Fig. 6. The visual results confirm our quantitative findings:
our model produces significantly cleaner and more accu-
rate segmentation masks for both unseen domains and novel
classes, whereas competing methods often suffer from seg-
mentation artifacts or missed detections.

5. Conclusion
We introduced SkySense-VITA, a unified in-context seg-
mentation model to synergistically handle optical and SAR
imagery using visual, textual, or fused prompts. Our
model addresses key prior limitations through two main
contributions: (1) a novel prompt-and-prediction decou-
pling architecture (VITA-Former and VITA-Decoder) for
robust, interference-free co-optimization, and (2) a progres-
sive training strategy featuring SGA for robust coarse-to-
fine learning. Supported by our new Sky-VT-300k dataset,
SkySense-VITA establishes a new SOTA on 18 segmenta-
tion tasks of remote sensing imagery. For future work, we
plan to extend our model to diverse Earth observation sen-
sors and temporal dynamics.
