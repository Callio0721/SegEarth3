Abstract
Weakly Supervised Semantic Segmentation (WSSS) lever-
ages Class Activation Maps (CAMs) to extract spatial infor-
mation from image-level labels. However, CAMs primarily
highlight the most discriminative foreground regions, lead-
ing to incomplete results. Prototype-based methods attempt
to address this limitation by employing prototype CAMs in-
stead of classifier CAMs. Nevertheless, existing prototype-
based methods typically use a single prototype for each
class, which is insufficient to capture all attributes of the
foreground features due to the significant intra-class vari-
ations across different images. Consequently, these meth-
ods still struggle with incomplete CAM predictions. In
this paper, we propose a novel framework called Proto-
typical Optimal Transport (POT) for WSSS. POT enhances
CAM predictions by dividing features into multiple clus-
ters and activating each cluster using its prototype. In
this process, a similarity-aware optimal transport is em-
ployed to assign features to the most probable clusters.
This similarity-aware strategy ensures the prioritization of
significant cluster prototypes, thereby improving the accu-
racy of feature assignment. Additionally, we introduce an
adaptive OT-based consistency loss to refine feature repre-
sentations. This framework effectively overcomes the lim-
itations of single-prototype methods, providing more com-
plete and accurate CAM predictions. Extensive experi-
mental results on standard WSSS benchmarks (PASCAL
VOC and MS COCO) demonstrate that our method signif-
icantly improves the quality of CAMs and achieves state-
of-the-art performances. The source code will be released
https://github.com/jianwang91/POT.
1. Introduction
Weakly supervised semantic segmentation (WSSS) with
image-level labels reduces the labor for pixel-level annota-
tions, thereby receiving increasing attention. In this task,
Class Activation Maps (CAMs) [59] are widely used to
†: Corresponding authors
localize objects and employed to generate pseudo-labels,
which are further utilized to train a segmentation model [8].
However, CAMs primarily highlight the most distinctive
foreground regions [36, 53], providing insufficient super-
vision for training the segmentation model [8].
To mitigate this limitation, prototype-based methods [9,
57, 58] are introduced. These methods first generate binary
masks for each class, either by feature similarity [9, 57] or
a pretrained segmentation model [58]. These binary masks
are then used to perform mask average pooling on the cor-
responding features to construct a prototype for each class
within an image. The resulting prototypes capture the es-
sential characteristics of foreground classes and are lever-
aged to activate features using cosine similarity. Compared
to classifier CAMs, these prototype CAMs are more com-
plete, thereby enhancing the overall performance.
However, prototype CAMs still fail to cover complete
regions of foreground objects in certain images. Feature
distribution of such images reveals that the foreground ob-
ject features are often sparse and dispersed (see Fig. 1 (a)).
This issue is primarily attributed to the significant intra-
class variations [37] in the dataset, which limits the abil-
ity of a single prototype to activate all relevant features.
Specifically, the classifier aims to capture global discrim-
inative intra-class characteristics to accurately classify an
image. Features that encapsulate such characteristics (e.g.,
faces within the person class) can be effectively recognized
and grouped to form a compact primary cluster. Neverthe-
less, regions belonging to the same class may exhibit highly
diverse appearances, even within the same image (see Fig 1
(a), areas in the red and green boxes). These image-specific
features cannot be closely clustered into the previously de-
scribed primary cluster due to their lack of global intra-class
characteristics. Instead, these feature clusters often appear
separately from the primary one. When constructing proto-
type CAMs with a single prototype, only features in close
proximity to the prototype are fully activated (red area in
Fig. 1 (a)), while other features are only partially activated
(green area in Fig. 1 (a)). This incomplete activation fails
to provide sufficient guidance for segmentation model train-
ing, leading to unsatisfactory performance.

To address the aforementioned challenge, we pro-
pose a novel plug-and-play framework called Prototypical
Optimal Transport (POT) for WSSS, as shown in Fig. 1 (b).
Considering the limitation of a single prototype in captur-
ing the diverse attributes of foreground features, POT di-
vides feature vectors into multiple clusters and generates
multiple cluster prototypes to encapsulate a broader range
of attributes within an image. By combining the activa-
tion results from each cluster, POT obtains comprehensive
foreground CAM results. Specifically, feature vectors with
confident CAM predictions are initially divided into mul-
tiple clusters. Each cluster encapsulates the most distinc-
tive characteristics of a class and serves as an anchor for
feature allocation within the image. Subsequently, a proto-
typical optimal transport allocation is performed to assign
each feature to the most appropriate cluster. Once the al-
location is completed, features are activated by their corre-
sponding cluster prototypes using cosine similarity. Finally,
the cluster-activated results of the same class are combined
to generate complete foreground CAM predictions.
In the above pipeline, accurately assigning features to
their corresponding clusters is both crucial and challenging.
The use of multiple clusters per class further increases the
complexity of the allocation process. Incorrectly assigned
features cannot be fully activated, resulting in incomplete
CAM predictions. To address this challenge, optimal trans-
port (OT) theory [39] is employed to learn an adaptive trans-
port plan for assigning features to their potential clusters
effectively. However, conventional OT employs an aver-
age constraint on target distribution, which treats all clus-
ter prototypes equally. This strategy overlooks the varying
significance and importance of different cluster prototypes,
potentially leading to suboptimal allocation. In contrast, we
introduce a similarity-aware constraint to address this lim-
itation. This constraint prioritizes cluster prototypes with
high similarities to the classifier CAM prototypes, recog-
nizing them as more critical. This bias allows the OT opti-
mization process to focus more on the prototypes containing
discriminative characteristics when determining the lowest
cost through the matching plan. Based on the transport plan,
an adaptive OT-based consistency loss is further proposed
to optimize the feature representation in POT. Our contribu-
tions can be summarized as follows:
• The reason for sparse and dispersed feature distribution
in current prototype-based methods is analyzed, reveal-
ing that a single prototype is insufficient to capture all
attributes of foreground features. This limitation leads to
incomplete foreground CAM predictions.
• A novel plug-and-play prototypical optimal transport
framework for WSSS is proposed. This framework em-
ploys optimal transport to effectively assign features to
multiple clusters. By combining cluster-activated results
of the same class, the framework enhances CAM predic-
tions on various existing WSSS methods.
• Our method achieves new state-of-the-art benchmarks in
multistage WSSS, attaining 76.1% and 76.7% mIoU on
the validation and test sets of Pascal VOC 2012 and
47.9% mIoU on the MS COCO 2014 validation set.

and accumulation [21, 22, 51], region growing [19, 55],

them, prototype-based methods [9, 10, 15, 37, 49] have
drawn increasing attention due to their effectiveness in en-
hancing CAM completeness. Chen et al. [9] introduce
image-specific prototypes by evaluating class-wise Inter-
section over Union (IoU) between the structure map and
classifier CAMs. Du et al. [15] propose a pixel-to-prototype
contrast method, which involves selecting top-K pixel fea-

Tang et al. [37] refine prototypes by aligning feature distri-
butions between context prototypes over the whole dataset
and instance prototypes of the current image. The generated
prototypes are further used to construct prototype CAMs
based on cosine similarity between them and feature vec-
tors. Despite their effectiveness, these prototype-construct
strategies generally rely on a single prototype per class to
activate features, which is inadequate given the huge intra-
class variations in the dataset. Unlike these approaches, our
method leverages multiple cluster prototypes to generate
prototype CAMs, providing a more comprehensive repre-
sentation of foreground classes. While Chen et al. [10] uti-
lize multiple clusters as well, their approach generates such
clusters across the dataset, relying on manually set thresh-
olds to select prototypes. This operation limits their perfor-
mance. In contrast, our method employs optimal transport
to learn an adaptive allocation strategy, leading to improved
results without the need for manual parameter tuning.
Optimal Transport. Optimal Transport (OT) [39] offers an
effective method for transferring mass between two distri-
butions. To reduce the computational cost of OT, Sinkhorn
OT [14, 23] is proposed, which can be efficiently solved
using the Sinkhorn algorithm. This algorithm ensures the
widespread applicability of OT among various fields. In
computer vision, OT has been successfully applied to many
different tasks, such as generative models [2, 56], structural
matching [45, 56], image matching [54], and domain adap-
tation [3, 4, 28, 50]. All these applications seek to find an
optimal plan that minimizes a specific cost function. In our
work, we employ optimal transport to create an allocation
plan that assigns features to the most appropriate clusters.
By learning such an adaptive transport plan [34], we im-
prove the efficiency and effectiveness of feature distribu-
tion, allowing for more precise feature clustering.
3. Methodology
This section presents a detailed explanation of the pro-
posed Prototypical Optimal Transport (POT) framework for
WSSS, as depicted in Fig. 2. The framework includes three
key stages: feature partitioning, feature allocation, and fea-
ture activation. In the first stage, feature vectors with con-
fident classifier CAM values are grouped into a fixed num-
ber of clusters for each class. These clusters capture the
most critical characteristics of their respective classes, and
a cluster prototype is generated for each of them to serve as
an anchor for the subsequent feature allocation. The second
stage employs Optimal Transport (OT) to allocate features
to clusters. The resulting transport plan provides the prob-
ability distribution for assigning each feature to a specific

cluster prototype established in the first stage, thereby guid-
ing the systematic allocation of feature vectors. In the final
stage, the cluster prototypes are updated based on the newly
allocated feature vectors, and features are activated indi-
vidually by calculating cosine similarities with their corre-
sponding cluster prototypes. In addition, an OT-based con-
sistency loss is introduced to maintain the consistency of the
final activation results and the classifier CAMs, which op-
timizes the feature representation and provides an effective
and bounded exploration of prototypes.

3.2. Feature Allocation
Following feature partitioning in Section 3.1, the next stage
involves allocating all the feature vectors to the previously
generated clusters. The key challenge in this stage is to ef-
fectively measure the distances between features and candi-
date prototypes. Common distance metrics, such as cosine
distance, typically focus on point-to-point solutions. These
methods calculate the distance between each feature and
prototype and then select the closest prototype for feature
allocation. However, these methods ignore the interactions
among features and treat each allocation individually. In
practice, feature allocation in this task should be consid-
ered a set-to-set problem, where the allocation of certain
features affects the subsequent allocation of others. To ad-
dress such a problem, Optimal Transport (OT) theory pro-
vides a comprehensive framework. We first briefly intro-
duce OT theory and then demonstrate its role in the fea-
ture allocation stage. Essentially, OT aims to optimize the
transfer of mass between two distributions under specified
marginal constraints [39]. Let M and N denote the lengths
of these two distributions. The cost matrix associated with
the transfer between these two distributions is represented
as C ∈ RM ×N . The OT problem can be defined as:

5. Conclusion
This paper introduces the POT framework, a novel approach
designed to address the issue of incomplete CAM predic-
tions in existing prototype-based approaches. We demon-
strate that a single prototype is insufficient to capture the
diverse attributes of foreground objects due to intra-class
variations, leading to incomplete CAMs. By utilizing multi-
ple feature clusters and a similarity-aware optimal transport
strategy, POT achieves precise feature allocation, obtaining
comprehensive and accurate results. Additionally, we in-
corporate an OT-based consistency loss to optimize feature
representations and constrain the prototype exploration, fur-
ther enhancing the effectiveness of the framework. Exten-
sive experiments on two popular datasets demonstrate the
superiority of our proposed method in image-level WSSS.
