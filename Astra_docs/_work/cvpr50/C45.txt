Abstract
Open-vocabulary semantic segmentation extends pixel-
level recognition to arbitrary text-described categories.
Despite strong global semantic understanding, vision-
language models such as CLIP exhibit limited spatial pre-
cision and semantic ambiguity across large vocabularies,
constraining their effectiveness for dense prediction. We
present S2C2Seg, a training-free framework that integrates
with existing methods through Category Subset Selection
(CSS) and Consistent Semantic Guidance (CSG). CSS em-
ploys multi-aspect scoring to filter candidate categories:
CLIP-based global semantic similarity, spatial presence
from dense prediction models, and cross-view consistency
via alignment and conditional entropy. This joint exploita-
tion of semantic, spatial, and consistency cues reduces cat-
egory redundancy and semantic ambiguity. CSG adap-

tively fuses CLIP global features with local spatial predic-
tions through category-specific confidence weighting, ap-

for correcting prediction biases while preserving spatial
precision for low-confidence categories. Extensive exper-

cability: when integrated with SCLIP , ProxyCLIP , and Cor-
rCLIP , S2C2Seg achieves consistent improvements of 3.4 to

the-art of 51.2% average mIoU.

strong zero-shot recognition across arbitrary text-specified
categories, yet extending it to open-vocabulary semantic
segmentation [30] remains challenging. CLIP’s contrastive
pretraining optimizes global image-text alignment, yielding

maps originally designed for classification, and overlapping
activations among semantically similar or co-occurring cat-

Existing methods address these challenges through two
primary directions. Spatial refinement approaches enhance

Despite improving spatial precision, these methods treat all
candidate categories uniformly regardless of semantic rela-
tionships or prediction confidence, propagating ambiguous
activations from global features to final predictions when
large vocabularies contain similar categories such as air-
plane and aircraft or co-occurring objects such as road and
vehicle. Disambiguation approaches [2, 18, 41] reduce se-
mantic overlap through similarity-based category ranking or
selection, constraining vocabulary size yet relying on global
similarity metrics without explicitly modeling spatial pre-
diction consistency. As these two directions target spatial
and semantic challenges respectively, the interdependence
between coarse localization and category overlap suggests
that joint modeling may yield superior results.
We propose S2C2Seg, a training-free framework coor-
dinating spatial localization and semantic disambiguation.
Image-level models like CLIP [31] provide robust global
semantics but lack spatial precision, while pixel-level mod-
els like CLIPSeg [26] enable fine-grained localization yet
yield inconsistent cross-category predictions. S2C2Seg rec-
onciles these complementary limitations via two coordi-
nated components: Category Subset Selection (CSS) fil-
ters candidate categories via multi-aspect scoring, reduc-
ing redundancy and ambiguity; Consistent Semantic Guid-
ance (CSG) fuses global semantic features with local spatial
predictions through category-specific confidence weighting,
with stronger regularization for semantically proximate cat-
egories while preserving spatial precision. Figure 1 illus-
trates improvements across eight benchmarks with repre-
sentative baselines.
Our primary contributions are as follows:
• We propose a training-free framework that integrates
global semantic models and local spatial predictors to
jointly address vocabulary disambiguation and spatial
refinement in open-vocabulary segmentation, achieving
consistent improvements across diverse baseline methods
without additional training.
• We introduce CSS, which filters candidate categories
through multi-aspect scoring that integrates global sim-
ilarity, spatial presence, and cross-view consistency, re-
ducing category redundancy and semantic ambiguity.
• We develop CSG, which adaptively fuses global seman-
tic features with local spatial predictions via category-
specific confidence weighting, applying stronger regular-
ization to semantically proximate categories while pre-
serving spatial precision.
2. Related Work
2.1. Open-Vocabulary Segmentation
Open-vocabulary semantic segmentation extends closed-
set segmentation to arbitrary text-described categories.
Training-based methodsenhance CLIP’s segmentation ca-
pability through region-text pseudo-labeling [8, 15], image-
level weak supervision [12, 14, 27], mask proposal gener-
ation [49, 50], and cross-modal distillation [24, 53], with
recent advances further addressing decoding efficiency and
cross-domain generalization [16, 23, 52, 56]. Despite strong
in-domain performance, these methods typically require
task-specific annotations.Training-free methodsuse pre-
trained VLMs without fine-tuning for improved general-
ization to novel categories. Representative strategies in-
clude self-attention refinement [10, 22, 25, 42] that modi-
fies CLIP’s internal attention for better spatial localization,
external prior integration from self-supervised or diffusion
models [21, 36, 40] for enhanced spatial detail, and en-
semble [3, 13] or global-local consistency strategies [43].
S2C2Seg addresses category space optimization through
multi-dimensional filtering and enhances prediction qual-
ity via category-aware adaptive fusion of global and local
features.
2.2. Category Selection Strategies
Category selection remains underexplored in OVSS. Exist-
ing methods rely on single-dimensional criteria: CaR [41]
refines categories via CLIP similarity, potentially under-
weighting those with strong local presence but weak global
alignment. FLOSS [2] uses text template entropy with pre-
defined templates, while CDAM [18] measures spatial at-
tention distribution through JS divergence for category rel-
evance. DenseCLIP [32] and ViewCo [33] explore dense
prediction and multi-view consistency; Mask-Adapter [24]
enforces mask IoU consistency but requires training. In
contrast, CSS integrates three complementary dimensions,
namely global semantic alignment, local spatial activation,
and prediction entropy, within a two-stage framework that
balances recall and precision for dynamic category filtering.
2.3. Semantic Guidance for Dense Prediction
Multiple approaches exploit CLIP’s semantic knowledge
for dense prediction: category-level modulation [18, 23,
32, 38] employs CLIP features as fixed semantic priors,
adaptive text guidance [44, 45] dynamically modulates em-
beddings conditioned on image context, and recent meth-

4. Experiments

Datasets and Metrics.We evaluate on eight benchmarks:
PASCAL VOC [11] (20/21 classes), PASCAL Context [28]
(59/60 classes), COCO-Object [4] (80 classes), COCO-

over-Union (mIoU, %) on validation sets.

in PyTorch on NVIDIA RTX 4090 GPUs. We adopt
ViT-B/16 CLIP [31] as the visual-language backbone and
CLIPSeg [26] for dense prediction. Following standard
practice [31], we use 80 prompt templates per category for
text embedding. For CSS, we set retention ratioτ= 0.3
and subset size boundsK min = 6,K max = 20. For CSG,

We compare five fusion strategies in Table 4: ad-

(+2.4). Figure 6 visualizes the complementary nature of

maintaining consistency when cues diverge (column 4).
4.4. Hyperparameter Sensitivity
We analyze three key hyperparameters on PASCAL VOC
validation set using ProxyCLIP baseline (Figure 7). The
fusion weightλbalances global semantic guidance with
local spatial predictions. Performance remains stable at
48.0–49.5% mIoU acrossλ∈ {0.4,0.8}, and we adopt
λ= 0.6. The retention ratioτdetermines initial category
set size before adaptive filtering, exhibiting robust perfor-
mance acrossτ∈ {0.2,0.5}withτ= 0.3balancing recall
and precision. The upper boundK max constrains final sub-
set size, showing consistent results forK max ∈ {15,25}.
We setK max = 20based on typical object distributions.
5. Conclusion
We present S2C2Seg, a training-free framework for open-
vocabulary semantic segmentation addressing category re-
dundancy and global–local semantic inconsistency. CSS
filters candidate categories via multi-aspect scoring to re-
duce redundancy and ambiguity while maintaining recall.
CSG adaptively fuses CLIP’s global semantic features with
CLIPSeg’s local spatial predictions via category-specific
confidence weighting. Experiments across eight bench-
marks show that integrating S2C2Seg with baselines such
as SCLIP, ProxyCLIP, and CorrCLIP consistently improves
performance by +3.4 to +9.7 pp mIoU, achieving state-of-
the-art 51.2% mIoU.
