Abstract
Semantic segmentation of ultra-high-resolution (UHR) re-
mote sensing imagery is critical for applications like envi-
ronmental monitoring and urban planning but faces compu-
tational and optimization challenges. Conventional meth-
ods either lose fine details through downsampling or frag-
ment global context via patch processing. While multi-
branch networks address this trade-off, they suffer from
computational inefficiency and conflicting gradient dynam-
ics during training. We propose F2Net, a frequency-aware
framework that decomposes UHR images into high- and
low-frequency components for specialized processing. The
high-frequency branch preserves full-resolution structural
details, while the low-frequency branch processes down-
sampled inputs through dual sub-branches capturing short-
and long-range dependencies. A Hybrid-Frequency Fu-
sion module integrates these observations, guided by two
novel objectives: Cross-Frequency Alignment Loss ensures
semantic consistency between frequency components, and
Cross-Frequency Balance Loss regulates gradient magni-
tudes across branches to stabilize training. Evaluated on
DeepGlobe and Inria Aerial benchmarks, F2Net achieves
state-of-the-art performance with mIoU of 80.22 and 83.39,
respectively.1

high-resolution (UHR) imagery has become increasingly
accessible, offering unprecedented spatial detail. While

2. Related Work
2.1. Segmentation
Semantic segmentation, a fundamental task in computer vi-
sion, aims to assign a categorical label to every pixel in an
image. The field was revolutionized by Fully Convolutional
Networks (FCN) [20], which first established the end-to-
end, pixel-to-pixel training paradigm. Following this break-
through, research diverged into several key strategies to ad-
dress the inherent challenges of the task.
One primary line of work focused on recovering spa-
tial details lost during encoding. U-Net [27] became the
archetype for this encoder-decoder approach, using skip-
connections to fuse deep, semantic features with shallow,
fine-grained features. This concept was further refined by
architectures like UNet++ [46], which introduced nested
and dense skip pathways to reduce the semantic gap be-
tween encoder and decoder feature maps.
A second strategy prioritized the aggregation of multi-
scale context, which FCNs struggled with due to their lim-
ited receptive fields. The DeepLab series [4] pioneered the
use of atrous convolution to capture features at multiple
scales without sacrificing spatial resolution. Concurrently,
PSPNet [44] introduced a pyramid pooling module to effec-
tively aggregate global context information.
A third major challenge was the poor localization of ob-
ject boundaries. Early methods combined CNN outputs
with post-processing steps like Conditional Random Fields
(CRFs) to refine edges [3]. Later, attention mechanisms
were integrated directly into the CNN architecture, such as
in Attention U-Net [23], which uses attention gates to sup-
press irrelevant regions while highlighting salient features
useful for the specific task.
More recently, the success of Vision Transformers
(ViT) [10] has instigated a shift away from pure CNNs.
Transformer-based models, such as SegFormer [38], lever-
age self-attention to effectively model long-range spatial de-
pendencies. Further advancing the field, Mask2Former [7]
proposed a novel mask classification paradigm, reformulat-
ing segmentation as a query-based set prediction problem.
2.2. Ultra High Resolution Segmentation
Standard segmentation methods struggle with UHR im-
ages due to memory constraints and structural complex-
ity. Early solutions—downsampling or patch-wise process-
ing—compromise global context or local detail [2, 45].
Recent advances adopt two strategies: Transformer-based
models and multi-branch architectures.
Transformer-based approaches leverage the long-range
dependency modeling capabilities of Transformers, often
with modifications like limited positional encodings and
lightweight decoders to manage memory overhead [38]. To
further integrate local details, hybrid CNN-Transformer ar-
chitectures have been proposed, combining the strengths
of both backbones [43]. Some hybrid models introduce
context-guided modules [14, 34] or patch-merging strate-
gies [31] to enhance boundary consistency by aggregating
information from neighboring patches. The multi-branch
paradigm separates global and local processing, typically
with a global branch handling downsampled inputs for con-
text and a local branch focusing on high-resolution patches
for detail [5, 12]. Extensions with multi-scale features, un-
certainty estimation [25], and locality-aware designs help
reduce boundary artifacts, but often increase latency due to
overlapping patch inference [16].
Yet, these methods rely on spatial partitioning (e.g.,
cropping or patches), which can disrupt object integrity, our
method enables simultaneous modeling of fine details and
global context without introducing spatial artifacts.
2.3. Representation Learning in Frequency Domain
Representing images in the frequency domain is an increas-
ingly effective technique in remote sensing, prized for its
ability to capture both global context and fine-grained de-
tails [24, 29, 35, 40, 42]. Methods such as wavelet de-
composition separate inputs into complementary low- and
high-frequency components to produce coherent predic-
tions [17]. Others employ adaptive frequency filtering to
sharpen inter-class boundaries [29] or use Fast Fourier Con-
volution to add global context, thereby improving the de-
tection of small objects [39]. Furthermore, transforms like
DCT facilitate robust feature matching across varying con-
ditions, such as illumination or seasonal shifts [42]. How-
ever, approaches that fuse multiple frequency components
face challenges with fusion strategies and training stabil-

3. Methodology
This section presents our novel framework; specific imple-
mentations are detailed in Sec. 4. As shown in Figure 1,
F2Net comprises high- and low-frequency branches, each
specialized for distinct spectral representations to simplify
learning and enhance task focus. A dynamic frequency de-
composition module first splits the input into high- and low-
frequency components. The high-frequency branch oper-
ates on full-resolution features to preserve fine structural de-
tails, while the low-frequency branch—capturing semanti-
cally rich but redundant signals—is downsampled to reduce
computation without sacrificing contextual integrity. The
low-frequency branch is further split into short- and long-
range sub-branches to model local and global dependen-
cies, respectively. A hybrid-frequency fusion (HFF) mod-
ule then integrates features across branches. To facilitate
multi-branch optimization, we introduce two objectives: (1)
Cross-Frequency Alignment Loss (CFAL) promotes seman-
tic consistency across frequencies; and (2) Cross-Frequency
Balance Loss (CFBL) equalizes gradient flow, ensuring sta-
ble and balanced training.

3.3. Low-Frequency Branch
For comprehensive low-frequency semantic modeling, the
downsampled low-frequency componentX LF is processed
through two complementary subbranches. The short-range
subbranch, built upon a dense convolutional block, produc-
ing an outputF s ∈R Hs×Ws×Cs. To address the limited
receptive field of CNNs, we introduce a long-range sub-
branch based on TinyViT [36], which processes the input
XLF to model long-range dependencies and generate global
feature representationsF l ∈R Hl×Wl×Cl.
3.4. Hybrid-Frequency Fusion

tain redundant or misaligned channels, which can hinder ef-

feature aggregation. Specifically, HFF first integrates the
outputs of the two low-frequency sub-branches to capture
both short- and long-range context, and then fuses this rep-
resentation with the high-frequency branch output to form
the final unified feature.

3.5. Cross-Frequency Loss & Optimization
Cross-Frequency Alignment Loss. In F2Net, each fre-
quency branch focuses on a distinct spectral component.
In specific, the high-frequency branch and low-frequency
branch are inherently asymmetric in both representation and
semantic abstraction. Although these branches target com-
plementary aspects of the image, they often encode differ-
ent representations of the same semantic content. Without
explicit alignment, this discrepancy may lead to semantic
inconsistency during feature fusion and segmentation. To
enforce coherent semantic understanding across high- and
low-frequency branches, we introduce a cross-frequency
alignment loss (CFAL). It encourages the branch outputs to
converge at the semantic level, ensuring that the same object
class is consistently represented across branches regardless
of frequency bias. We implement CFAL using a symmetric
Kullback–Leibler (KL) divergence, defined as:

4. Experiment
4.1. Implementation & Evaluation Protocol
Dataset. We evaluate our method on two benchmarks.
DeepGlobe[9] contains 803 satellite images (2448×2448),
split into 455/207/142 for training/validation/testing, with
annotations for seven land-cover classes (urban, agricul-
ture, rangeland, forest, water, barren, unknown), where
the unknown class is excluded [12].Inria Aerial[21] in-
cludes 180 aerial images (5000×5000) annotated with bi-
nary building masks, following the split 126/27/27 for train-
ing/validation/testing as in [16].
Implementation Details.The short-range subbranch is im-
plemented using DeepLabv3 [4] with a customized ResNet-
18 backbone. The long-range subbranch adopts a 6-layer
ViT-tiny architecture [10]. All backbones are initialized
with weights pre-trained on ImageNet-1K. The Mamba
backbone is built upon VMamba-Tiny-M2 [19], featuring
a hierarchical structure of four stages with depths [2, 2, 4,
2], and a base channel dimension 64. We set the channel
grouping factorNin Eq. 3 to 8. We optimize all parameters
using SGD with momentum 0.9, an initial learning rate of
1×10 −3, and polynomial decay (power 0.9). Loss weights
are set asλ 1 =λ 2 = 0.1,λ 3 = 1. Training runs for 40k
iterations on Inria Aerial and 80k on DeepGlobe, with batch
size 8 on a DGX-1 workstation (Tesla V100 GPUs).
Baselines & metrics.We compare F2Net with state-
of-the-art methods in two groups:(i) Conventional seg-
mentation—FCN-8s [20], UNet [27], DeepLabv3+ [4],
STDC [11], SegFormer [38], Mask2Former [7], and
PointRend [15];(ii) UHR-specific—CascadePSP [8],
PPN [37], MagNet [13], GLNet [5], FCtL [16], ISD-
Net [12], SGNet [34], GPWFormer [14], RUE [25], and
BPT [31]. We report accuracy via F1 and mIoU, and effi-
ciency via FPS and memory. All FPS are measured on a
single NVIDIA Tesla V100 for fair comparison.

5. Conclusion
We present F2Net, a frequency-aware framework for se-
mantic segmentation of UHR remote sensing images. De-
composing inputs into high- and low-frequency components
allows F2Net to process structural details and semantics in
parallel via specialized branches. The Hybrid-Frequency
Fusion module integrates multi-scale features, while cross-
frequency objectives stabilize training. Experiments on two
widely-used benchmarks -DeepGlobeandInria Aerial-
show state-of-the-art results.
