Abstract
Remote sensing image segmentation is essential for appli-
cations such as natural disaster monitoring and precision
agriculture. Open-vocabulary segmentation improves flexi-
bility by removing fixed category constraints, enabling more
fine-grained scene understanding. However, unlike CLIP’s
pretraining objective that emphasizes global image–text
alignment, segmentation requires discriminative patch-level
representations for accurate pixel-wise prediction. Con-
sequently, the quality of attention maps in the final trans-
former layers is critical for modeling interactions among
spatial regions. Existing methods often produce subop-
timal representations when capturing the complex spatial
structures of remote sensing imagery. To address this is-
sue, we refine CLIP’s attention mechanism through three
modifications: (1) replacing patch-to-patch attention with
intermediate-layer feature similarities to better preserve
spatial structure; (2) leveraging intermediate-layer atten-
tion for class-to-patch alignment to reduce classification
interference; and (3) disabling the [CLS] token’s self-
attention to mitigate bias. Experiments on multiple remote
sensing benchmarks, including building and road extrac-
tion datasets, show that our method achieves state-of-the-
art performance among training-free approaches.

With the advancement of training-free segmentation

datasets by leveraging large-scale pre-trained models, offer-

made in certain fields, the dependence of most models on

methods in real-world scenarios. To address this shortcom-

widespread attention in recent years.
CLIP’s image-text pre-training excels in overall image
understanding, but its operations are at the image level,
which fundamentally misaligns with the pixel-level dis-
criminative features required for segmentation. This dis-
crepancy is particularly prominent in the remote sensing
field, where complex scene distributions, extreme scale
variations, and heterogeneous landscapes demand fine-
grained feature representations. Current adaptation meth-
ods typically use attention mechanisms to derive dense fea-
tures, generating token embeddings by re-calculating the
query-key (RCS) or key-key (SCSA) attention map [19].
By treating attention maps as monolithic entities and focus-
ing on restructuring rather than component-wise analysis,
these methods fail to balance global semantics and local de-
tails, leading to suboptimal representations for remote sens-
ing imagery.
In this paper, we innovatively conduct a comprehensive
analysis of CLIP’s attention mechanisms for remote sensing
segmentation, decomposing their influence into three inter-
connected dimensions (As show in Fig. 1): (1) the similar-
ity calculation between patch tokens, modeling inter-region
relationships; (2) the composition of the[CLS] token, cap-
turing global information; and (3) the [CLS] token’s con-
tribution to patch tokens, revealing its impact on local re-
gions during segmentation. Our method strategically re-
calibrates these elements, enabling precise open-vocabulary
segmentation without training, while directly addressing the
inherent complexity of remote sensing.
In summary, our contributions are as follows:
• We provide the first detailed analysis of how CLIP’s at-
tention map components impact remote sensing image
segmentation, focusing on three key elements: patch to-
ken similarity, the[CLS] token composition, and its con-
tribution to patch tokens.
• We propose a novel approach to effectively extract high-
quality dense features from CLIP’s attention map for
pixel-level image segmentation, addressing the challenge
of transforming CLIP’s image-level features into dense,
pixel-wise representations.
• We conducted experiments on ten public remote sensing
datasets, achieving state-of-the-art performance with an
average gain of 1.7% on open-vocabulary segmentation
and 1.1% on object extraction tasks.
2. Related Work
2.1. Vision-Language Model
Vision-language models [6, 10, 15, 27] establish connec-
tions between visual and textual modalities, and these
models are typically pretrained on large-scale image-text
datasets [29]. Among these models, CLIP is one of the most
widely adopted. CLIP is a model trained using contrastive
learning on a large collection of image-text pairs, enabling
it to perform zero-shot retrieval tasks effectively. Specifi-
cally, the classification result is obtained by computing the
similarity between the output [CLS] token of the vision
encoder and the embedding produced by the text encoder.
However, due to the limitations of its pretraining strategy,
the model can only perform image-level alignment with
text. It cannot be directly applied to pixel-level alignment
tasks, such as segmentation or object detection. Many ex-
isting methods [31, 36] have attempted to modify the CLIP
model to enhance its fine-grained feature extraction capa-
bilities while preserving its strong generalization ability.
2.2. Open-Vocabulary Natural Image Segmentation
Open-vocabulary segmentation aims to detect and label im-
age regions based on a list of vocabulary terms. Apply-
ing CLIP to dense prediction tasks presents challenges.
MaskCLIP [11, 43] highlights that CLIP’s image features
are primarily determined by the value (V) component in
the attention mechanism, proposing to replace the query
(Q) and key (K) matrices with a 1 ×1 convolution. While
CLIP’s direct output for segmentation often leads to subop-
timal performance, it still provides useful guidance. CLIP
Surgery [21] suggests refining these coarse predictions us-
ing prompts like points or boxes, which are then input into
segmentation models like SAM [17].
SCLIP [32] analyzes CLIP’s final attention block and
shows that query-query similarity improves dense visual
representations by capturing relationships between patch to-
kens of the same category. NACLIP [13] refines this with

2.3. Open-Vocabulary Remote Sensing Image Seg-
mentation
Although numerous methods have adapted CLIP for seg-
mentation, most are developed for natural images, as CLIP
is pretrained on large-scale natural image–text pairs. When
applied to remote sensing imagery, these approaches often
suffer from severe domain discrepancies. To address this is-
sue, several training-based open-vocabulary remote sensing
segmentation methods, such as OVRS [5] and GSNet [41],
have been proposed to improve performance through task-
specific learning. In contrast, SegEarth-OV [20] attempts to
bridge the gap between CLIP pretraining and downstream
segmentation by introducing a learnable upsampling mod-
ule and a heuristic strategy to reduce the influence of the
[CLS] token on patch tokens. However, existing methods
generally treat the attention map as a whole without analyz-
ing its internal components. In this work, we conduct an
in-depth analysis of the attention computation process and
introduce targeted refinements for each component of the
attention map.

distinct components and analyze the influence of each on

further propose targeted optimization strategies to enhance
the quality of the resulting dense features. Following the
setup of SegEarth-OV [20], we also use SimFeatUp as the
upsampling module. The overview is shown in Fig. 2.

4.2. Patch-to-Patch Similarity
For a standard ViT, the attention map is 197×197, and
removing the first row and column for the [CLS] token
yields a 196×196 matrix of patch-patch similarities. Re-
cent works have proposed strategies to improve these maps:
SegEarth-OV combines Query-Query, Key-Key, and Value-
Value similarities via a weighted sum for enhanced atten-
tion:
A = α·(QQ⊤) + β·(KK ⊤) + γ·(V V ⊤), (8)
where Q, K, V denote the query, key, and value matrices,
and α, β, γ are weighting coefficients.

metric structure, which avoids discrepancies introduced by
separate projections in standard Query-Key attention. This
reduces projection-induced bias and enhances the reliabil-
ity of token relationships, which is particularly beneficial
for dense prediction tasks such as segmentation.

4.3. CLS Token Reconstruction
When adapting CLIP to dense prediction tasks, the [CLS]
token is typically discarded, and only patch tokens are used.
However, during pretraining, patch tokens interact with the
[CLS] token, leading to patch tokens encoding significant
global context. To mitigate this, SegEarth-OV subtracts the
final-layer [CLS] token embedding from each patch token
to remove global bias. In fact, patch tokens also carry infor-
mation from other categories during this attention compu-
tation process. The [CLS] token in the final layer carries
only a small amount of category information and does not
encompass information from all categories.

5. Experiment
5.1. Datasets and Metrics
To validate the effectiveness of the proposed method, we
conduct evaluations on three tasks: open-vocabulary se-
mantic segmentation of remote sensing images, building ex-
traction, and road extraction. Specifically, we evaluate our
method on eight publicly available datasets for the open-
vocabulary segmentation task, and on one dataset each for
the building and road extraction tasks. Overall, our method
is assessed on ten datasets across three distinct tasks.
Datasets. For the open-vocabulary segmentation task,
we select eight publicly available datasets, namely Open-
EarthMap [37], LoveDA [33], iSAID [35], Potsdam, Vai-
hingen, UA Vid [24], UDD5 [7], and VDD [4]. For building
extraction and road extraction tasks, we selected one dataset
for each, namely WHUSAT.II [14] and Massachusetts [25].
Metrics. We follow prior work [22] by adopting the
Intersection-over-Union (IoU) as the evaluation metric and
apply it consistently across all models.
5.2. Implementation Details
We adopt ViT/B as the backbone model. All input images
are resized to 448×448 before being fed into the CLIP im-
age encoder. During inference, we apply a sliding window
strategy to preserve model performance. Specifically, we
use a window size of 224×224 to slide across the image
and then stitch the predicted patches together to form the
final segmentation result of the original image. For the text
input, we follow OpenAI’s approach by averaging the em-
beddings generated from 80 different templates. For the pa-
rameter l, we define it to be between layers 6 and 9, its value
is determined for each dataset based on the entropy map of
the [CLS] token. λ0 is set to 1, andλi for i∈{1,2, 3}is set
to 0.4.K contains layers 9 to 11. The parameter α is set to
0.2. All experiments were conducted on a single NVIDIA
V100 GPU.

6. Conclusion
We introduce a novel framework for open-vocabulary seg-
mentation of remote sensing images by utilizing atten-
tion maps derived from CLIP. A comprehensive analy-
sis is conducted to examine the impact of various com-
ponents within CLIP’s attention mechanism on segmenta-
tion accuracy, along with a methodology to extract high-
quality dense features for pixel-level segmentation. Specif-
ically, we investigate the evolution of attention patterns
across network layers and their role in enhancing spatial lo-
calization. Extensive experiments on multiple benchmark
datasets demonstrate that our method achieves state-of-the-
art performance and exhibits strong generalization across
diverse data sources. The effectiveness and robustness of
attention-based strategies for efficient remote sensing im-
age segmentation are thereby validated.
