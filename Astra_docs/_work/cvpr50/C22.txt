Abstract
Multi-center star shape is a prevalent object shape feature,
which has proven effective in model-based image segmen-
tation methods. However, the shape field function induced
by the multi-center star shape is non-smooth, and directly
applying it to the data-driven image segmentation network
architecture design may lead to instability in backpropaga-
tion. This paper proposes a convex combination star (CCS)
shape, possessing multi-center star shape properties, and
has the advantage of effectively controlling the shape of the
region through a smooth field function. The sufficient con-
dition of the proposed CCS shape can be combined into the
image segmentation neural network structure design through
the bridge between the variational segmentation model and
the activation function of the data-driven method. Taking
Segment Anything Model (SAM) and its improved version as
backbone networks, we have shown that the segmentation
network architecture with CCS shape properties can greatly
improve the accuracy of segmentation results.
1. Introduction
Image segmentation is essential in medical and remote sens-
ing fields. Unlike natural images, objects in medical and
remote sensing images exhibit distinct geometric shape, so a
satisfactory segmentation result should maintain these char-
acteristics. Some task-specific traditional methods, such
as graph cuts and active contours [ 1, 3, 20, 27], improve
segmentation accuracy by incorporating shape and other
priors [2, 13, 30, 31, 33, 35]. The star-shaped prior, in partic-
ular, has shown effective in various studies [ 14, 24, 36].
*Corresponding author
Vicente [36] introduced a single-center star-shaped con-
straint within an interactive graph-cut framework, and later
extended by Gulshan et al. [14] and Isack et al. [18] to multi-
center and hedgehog shape models, respectively. However,
the interactive graph-cut framework restricts parallel process-
ing, posing efficiency challenges. Additionally, traditional
methods typically rely on manually selected low-level fea-
tures, such as edges, textures, and colors, making it difficult
to capture high-level semantic information in complex im-
ages. In contrast, deep learning methods [29, 32, 34, 39] can
learn more complex and abstract features from large-scale
annotated data in an end-to-end manner.
Currently, many studies have integrated shape, bound-
ary, and other priors into neural networks [5, 6, 11, 15, 23],
generally falling into two categories: one is adding regular-
ization terms to the loss function [9, 15, 17, 23], while the
other is integrating prior information directly into the net-
work architecture via specialized sub-networks [5, 37, 38].
However, regularized-loss-function methods only enforce
shape priors during training, with no theoretical guarantee
that the network’s output retains desired shape properties
during prediction. Thus, methods integrating prior informa-
tion directly into the architecture deserve more attention. For
instance, Chen et al. [5] refined segmentation predictions
by learning directional maps, while works [6, 37] used pre-
dicted vertices or contour points to improve segmentation.
Other approaches [11, 38] combine boundary information
to enhance model performance. However, such methods
are often directly implemented by adding network layers
based on engineering experience, lacking mathematical in-
terpretability.

ods require backpropagation of gradients during training. It

To integrate multi-center shape priors into data-driven im-
age segmentation approaches, we develop a novel star shape

ing multi-center star shape properties, and controllable by a
smooth flow field. Thus, it can be seamlessly incorporated
into variational models and data-driven method. By dual

problem incorporating the CCS shape constraint. This en-

through this module during forward propagation, thereby
achieving an interpretable fusion of the shape prior field with
the segmentation output. Furthermore, we design a novel
shape loss function based on CCS shape constraint, which
can be directly integrated into the network’s loss function to
enhance segmentation performance.

• We present a variational model and its dual algorithm

• Through the mathematical connection between the activa-

2.2. Star shape
The concept of star-shaped regions was first introduced by
Veksler [36], defining a region as star-shaped with respect to
a center c if any ray originating from c intersects the bound-
ary exactly once. To ensure consistency in expression, we
write the star shape in the following mathematical definition:
Definition 1 (star shape [36]) Let S ⊆ R2 be a bounded
closed region. Given a center c ∈ S, the region S is called
star shape w.r.tc if the line segment {y | y = (1 − λ)x +
λc, λ ∈ (0, 1)} ⊆ S for all x ∈ S.
In [36], the star-shaped prior was expressed as a discrete
energy function which can be optimized using graph cut
methods. In [22], the authors extended this star-shaped con-
straint to a continuous formulation. For a smooth indicator
function u : Ω → [0, 1], they constrained u to the following
set Pc, enforcing a continuous star-shaped condition:
Pc = {u : ⟨∇u(x), c − x⟩ ⩾ 0, a.e.∀x ∈ Ω}. (3)
This star-shaped constraint, however, relies on a single-
center point, while objects in real images often do not follow
a single-center star shape. A natural extension is to use
multiple points to define a more general star-shaped region.
Gulshan et al. [14] extended the definition of single-center
star-shaped to multi-center star-shaped, defined as follows:
Definition 2 (multi-center star shape [14]) Let S ⊆ R2
be a bounded closed region. Given a closed set of star cen-
ters C ⊆ S, the region S is called multi-center star shape
w.r.tC if ∀x ∈ S, ∃c(x) ∈ arg min

incorporated this multi-center star-shaped constraint into in-

non-smooth when using continuous field conditions to con-
trol the shape. This non-smooth field further leads to issues
in the backpropagation. Therefore, in the next section, we
introduce a new star shape, allowing the multi-center star
shape prior constraint to be integrated into neural networks.

3.2. Convex combination star shape constraint
From the definition of the CCS shape, it can be seen that
most objects and segmentation tasks conform to this shape.
We detail this property as Theorem 1.
Theorem 1 (universal shape prior) Let S ⊆ R2 be a
bounded closed region, and let ∂S denote its boundary
set. Then, S is a CCS shape region with respect to ∂S
when ε → 0+.
Theorem 1 states that all objects are star-shaped with mul-
tiple centers at their boundaries, and when ε is sufficiently
small, they are also CCS shapes.
Directly applying the definition to enforce the CCS shape
constraint requires calculating the line segment from each
foreground pixel to its corresponding center point. Inspired
by [22], we present in Theorem 2 a sufficient condition for
CCS shapes, which makes the constraint more computation-
ally efficient to handle.
Theorem 2 (sufficient condition) Let u ∈ C1(Ω), and as-
sume superlevel set u−1[γ, +∞) is closed with a simple,
closed boundary curve. Further, assume the closed set
C ⊆ u−1[γ, +∞). Define the set P as :
P = {u : ⟨∇u(x), s(x)⟩ ⩾ 0, a.e.∀x ∈ Ω}. (10)
where s(x) = cε(x) − x. If u ∈ P, then any superlevel
set u−1[γ, +∞) is a CCS shape region with respect to set C
when ε → 0+.
Proofs of the theorems could be found in Appendix B.

Different from the existing sub-network structures designed

4.2. Datasets and preprocessing
We evaluate our method and model on the following three
medical imaging datasets and one remote sensing dataset.
ISIC 2018 dataset [10]: This dataset contains 2,594 high-
resolution skin lesion images with segmentation annotations.
We use 1,868 images for training, 467 for validation, and the
remaining 259 for testing. All images are padded to a square
format and resized to 512×512.
Refuge dataset [25]: This dataset consists of 1,200 im-
ages, evenly divided into training, validation, and test sets
for optic cup and optic disc segmentation. Here, we focus
only on optic cup segmentation, with all images cropped to
512×512 around the ROI region.
Kvasir dataset [26]: The Kvasir dataset includes 1,000
endoscopy images with polyp segmentation annotations. We
divide it into 800 images for training, 100 for validation, and
100 for testing. All images are resized to 512×512.
WHU Building dataset [19]: This dataset contains 8,189
images at 512×512 resolution, captured from Christchurch,
New Zealand. It is split into 4,736 images for training, 1,036
for validation, and 2,416 for testing.
The vector field sg is computed from the set of center
points C in the ground truth, and we propose two straight-
forward methods to obtain C. For relatively simple-shaped
medical images, we uniformly sample 30 points along the
boundary. For remote sensing images with more complex
shapes, we employ morphological methods to extract points
from the convex and concave regions of the image.

5. Conclusion
In this work, we introduce the convex combination star
(CCS) shape and demonstrate the generality of this shape.
To incorporate this shape prior into image segmentation mod-
els, we propose the sufficient condition for preserving CCS
shape. Additionally, we develop a solution algorithm for
the model integrating CCS prior. Since the solution of the
Algorithm 1 takes the form of a sigmoid function, we unroll
the algorithm into network modules, replacing the conven-
tional sigmoid activation function in neural networks. Thus,
the shape information can be effectively integrated with im-
age features via the CCS module. In this paper, we take
SAM and SAM2 as an example to show that the proposed
methods are very effective. Please refer to the appendix for
experimental results on other convolutional networks.
Our approach could be further improved. Theoretically,
whether the field conditions can still maintain the CCS when
ε does not tend to 0 remains unproven mathematically. In
practice, the accurate prediction of the CCS field can be
achieved by better designing the predictive network template.
We believe that this common shape prior is highly beneficial
for precise segmentation in medical, remote sensing, and
natural images. It can guide the network to quickly learn the
properties of objects.
