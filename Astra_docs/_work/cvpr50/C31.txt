Abstract
Open-vocabulary part segmentation (OVPS) aims to seg-
ment objects into fine-grained parts while generalizing
to unseen categories. Existing VLM-based methods face
two challenges: (1) object over-segmentation, caused by
overly broad semantic activations, and (2) part under-
segmentation, resulting from weak fine-grained percep-
tion. To address these issues, we propose HOPS, a two-
stage framework for hierarchical open-vocabulary part
segmentation. HOPS introduces a bidirectional seman-
tic–structural attention fusion mechanism that integrates
CLIP’s semantic alignment with DINO’s structural per-
ception. In the object segmentation stage, the Attention-
Aware Filtering Module (AFM) refines cross-modal simi-
larity maps via semantic–structural attention to suppress
object over-segmentation. In the part segmentation stage,
the Affinity-Guided Enhancement Module (AEM) iteratively
propagates part responses to progressively expand activa-
tion regions, effectively mitigating part under-segmentation.
Experiments on Pascal-Part-116, ADE20K-Part-234, and
PartImageNet demonstrate that HOPS achieves state-of-
the-art performance with superior generalization. Our code
is available at https://github.com/TJU-IDVLab/HOPS.
1. Introduction
Part segmentation aims to decompose an object into fine-
grained semantic components. Open-vocabulary part seg-
mentation (OVPS) is an emerging task in computer vision.
Unlike traditional part segmentation [19, 43, 44], OVPS
[7, 8, 26, 27, 47] requires models not only to segment part
categories seen during training, but also to generalize to
novel categories. OVPS has significant application value
in fields such as medical image analysis [3, 39, 48], image
editing [2, 23, 30], and robotic manipulation [20, 56].

Although not all OVPS methods explicitly perform ob-
ject segmentation, accurate object-level perception is cru-
cial for capturing spatial structure and guiding part rea-
soning. However, current models often suffer from ob-
ject over-segmentation (see Fig. 1). This issue stems from
the training objective of VLMs (e.g. CLIP [36], ALIGN
[24]), which prioritize global image–text alignment over
fine-grained, pixel-level correspondence [36, 42, 57]. While
CLIP’s cross-modal similarity maps capture semantically
related regions, their weak spatial precision often pro-
duces overly broad activations, leading to object over-
segmentation (see Sec. 3.1 for details). To address this,
we propose the Attention-Aware Filtering Module (AFM),
which incorporates a bidirectional semantic–structural at-
tention fusion mechanism between CLIP and DINO to re-
fine the cost map (i.e. cross-modal similarity map) without
pixel-level supervision. AFM leverages the complementary
strengths of both models: CLIP’s attention highlights se-
mantically related regions but includes structurally irrele-
vant areas (e.g. when segmenting a cow, CLIP may pro-
duce high responses on the meadow due to their frequent
co-occurrence during training), whereas DINO’s attention
focuses on fine-grained structural cues such as boundaries
and topology. Through bidirectional fusion, DINO con-
strains CLIP’s attention to suppress connections that are se-
mantically related but structurally inconsistent, while CLIP
guides DINO to focus on semantically meaningful regions.
This process yields a semantic–structural attention, which
is used to enhance activations within target object regions
and suppress false responses in irrelevant areas, thereby al-
leviating object over-segmentation.
Existing OVPS methods also suffer from part under-
segmentation (Fig. 1). This issue arises from the limited
fine-grained semantic understanding of VLMs, which strug-
gle to accurately localize small parts within the entire im-
age [8, 47]. Without explicit spatial constraints, the model
produces weak activations for small parts (see Fig. 2). Al-
though hierarchical frameworks such as PBAPS [27] alle-
viate this issue by performing part segmentation within ob-
ject regions, they still fail to achieve complete part coverage
due to weak initial part responses. To address part under-
segmentation, we propose the Affinity-Guided Enhance-
ment Module (AEM). AEM builds a semantic–structural
affinity matrix by fusing CLIP and DINO attentions, and
employs an adaptive iterative enhancement mechanism to
propagate salient part responses toward weakly activated
target regions. This process progressively expands part ac-
tivation areas while maintaining semantic coherence, thus
effectively alleviating part under-segmentation. Integrated
into the Hierarchical Open-V ocabulary Part Segmentation
(HOPS) framework, AEM significantly improves the com-
pleteness and accuracy of part segmentation.
The contributions of this work are threefold: (i) We
systematically analyze two challenges in OVPS—object
over-segmentation and part under-segmentation. (ii) By
leveraging the complementarity between CLIP’s cross-
modal semantic alignment and DINO’s structural percep-
tion, we propose AFM and AEM to mitigate object over-
segmentation and part under-segmentation, respectively.
(iii) Extensive experiments on multiple benchmark datasets
demonstrate that HOPS achieves state-of-the-art perfor-
mance with superior generalization to unseen categories.
2. Related Work
Open-Vocabulary Semantic Segmentation.Existing
OVSS methods primarily follow two paradigms. The two-
stage paradigm [12, 15, 17, 52] first generates class-agnostic
masks and then classifies each mask individually using
VLMs [24, 25, 36]. For instance, ODISE [51] extracts im-
age features via Stable Diffusion [37] and employs a mask
generator [5, 28] to produce candidate masks, which are
classified by CLIP. However, the lack of semantic guid-
ance during mask generation often yields imprecise masks.
The one-stage paradigm [6, 53, 55] directly calculates im-
age–text similarity for pixel-wise classification. TagCLIP
[29] omits the final self-attention layer of CLIP to re-
tain spatial detail and employs patch-level classification
with dual-masking attention refinement and category re-
identification. CATSeg [6] builds a cost map via cross-
modal similarity and introduces spatial and class aggrega-
tion modules to improve semantic consistency and category
discrimination. Our HOPS builds upon the cost aggregation
of CATSeg. In addition, some methods [1, 21, 46] synthe-
size visual prototypes using generative models [13, 14, 37],
enabling training-free OVSS via prototype matching.
Open-Vocabulary Part Segmentation.OVPS [9, 10,
45, 49] requires modeling structural dependencies between
parts and objects, demanding more powerful feature rep-
resentations and a deeper understanding of object struc-
tures. OPS [35] adopts a class-agnostic, object-aware train-
ing strategy that improves segmentation accuracy on un-
seen categories. VLPart [40] employs multi-granularity
joint training and leverages dense semantic correspon-
dences extracted by DINO [34] for novel part parsing. OV-
PARTS [47] introduces the Pascal-Part-116 and ADE20K-
Part-234 benchmarks and establishes baseline models under
both two-stage and one-stage paradigms. PartCLIPSeg [7]
parses object-part names to construct universal part embed-
dings and employs attention mechanisms to strengthen acti-
vation of small parts. PartCATSeg [8] proposes a decoupled
cost aggregation strategy, improving part perception with
DINO structure guidance. In addition, some methods adopt
a hierarchical paradigm to explicitly model object–part rela-
tionships. For instance, ViRReq [41] models whole–part re-
lations via a hierarchical knowledge base, while PartGLEE
[26] uses Q-Former to decompose objects into constituent

3. Problem Analysis
3.1. Object Over-Segmentation
As shown in Fig. 3, CLIP produces coarse similarity maps,
whose activations often spill into neighboring regions be-
yond the target object, causing object over-segmentation.
To quantify object over-segmentation, we introduce the
Over-Segmentation Index (OSI), defined as the proportion
of the predicted mask exceeding the ground-truth relative
to the predicted area. A higher OSI indicates more severe
object over-segmentation. We conduct comparative experi-
ments on PASCAL VOC [16], where 20 object classes are
divided into 15 seen classes (available during training) and
5 unseen classes (absent during training). Evaluation met-
rics include mIoU, mOSI (mean OSI), and h-IoU (harmonic
mean of seen/unseen mIoU).
The results in Tab. 1 show that supervised training in (II)
notably improves mIoU on seen classes and reduces mOSI
by 19.55% compared with (I), proving that supervised train-
ing partially mitigates object over-segmentation. However,
for unseen classes, (II) yields lower mIoU and higher mOSI,
indicating that supervised training weakens CLIP’s inherent
zero-shot generalization and aggravates over-segmentation
on unseen classes. Thus, relying solely on supervised train-
ing is inadequate to address object over-segmentation in
open-vocabulary scenarios. It is worth noting that although
unseen classes often exhibit under-segmentation, this does
not conflict with over-segmentation. Under-segmentation
indicates incomplete coverage of the ground-truth, whereas
over-segmentation corresponds to false coverage of irrele-
vant regions, and both can coexist. Incorporating AFM into
(I) and (II)—yielding (III) and (IV), respectively— reduces
object over-segmentation and improves segmentation accu-
racy, regardless of whether supervised training is applied.
This confirms the effectiveness of AFM in alleviating ob-
ject over-segmentation in open-vocabulary scenarios.
3.2. Part Under-Segmentation
As shown in Fig. 2, CLIP’s part perception strongly depends
on spatial constraints. Without explicit object masks as pri-
ors, the model fails to activate small parts, resulting in part
under-segmentation. To quantify this issue, we introduce
the Under-Segmentation Index (USI), defined as the pro-
portion of the ground-truth region that is not covered by the
predicted mask relative to the ground-truth area. A larger
USI indicates more severe part under-segmentation.

[47] with the results in Tab. 2. Compared with the one-
stage method (I), the two-stage method (II) incorporates
object-level spatial constraints, improving mIoU and alle-

tance of object priors for accurate part perception. Integrat-
ing AFM (III) refines object masks, providing more reli-
able spatial constraints and thereby increasing mIoU while
reducing mUSI. With the addition of AEM (IV), part re-
sponses are iteratively enhanced, achieving the best overall
performance. These results demonstrate that combining ob-

4. Method
4.1. Preliminary
Problem Definition.OVPS [7, 8, 26, 27, 47] aims to
segment part categories unseen during training. Notably,
part categories are tightly coupled with their parent object
classes, parts with the same name but from different ob-
ject classes are distinct categories (e.g. “cat tail” and “dog
tail”). During training, the model learns from a base set of
object–part categoriesC seen (e.g. “dog head”, “bird wing”)
with mask annotations. During inference, the model must
assign each pixel a label from the open setC=C seen ∪
Cunseen, whereC unseen denotes part categories that are unseen
during training. Specifically,C unseen includes both new parts
of known objects (e.g. “dog belly”) and parts of novel ob-
jects (e.g. “panda head”).
Cost Computation and Embedding.We use the image
and text encoders of CLIP [36] to extract dense visual em-
beddings and text embeddings of categories, respectively.
Cosine similarity between these embeddings is computed to
obtain the initial cost map [6, 8], which measures the corre-
spondence between each image location and category. The
cost map is then convolved into cost embeddings, which
serve as input to subsequent aggregation modules. Further
details are provided in the supplementary material.
Cost Aggregation.Leveraging image spatial continuity
and inter-class semantic correlations, CATSeg [6] performs
dual aggregation on cost embeddings.Spatial aggregation:
For each class channel, it independently uses Swin Trans-
former [31] to model spatial dependencies through local and
global window attention, enhancing prediction consistency
within target regions.Class aggregation:Positional infor-
mation along the class dimension is omitted, and a trans-
former without positional encoding is employed to capture
inter-class relationships, effectively suppressing semantic
ambiguity and noisy activations.
DINO Structural Guidance.While CLIP excels at cross-
modal semantic alignment, its attention primarily focuses
on global semantics, limiting precise part localization. In
contrast, DINO [34] effectively captures intra-image geo-
metric structures and semantic context. Building on this,
PartCATSeg [8] introduces DINO’s self-supervised features
as structural guidance by concatenating them with cost em-
beddings, which serve as the query and key in the spatial
aggregation module (see supplementary material). Our pro-
posed HOPS further explores the complementarity between
CLIP and DINO by fusing DINO’s structural attention with
CLIP’s semantic attention in both AFM and AEM, pre-
serving semantic alignment while enhancing local structural
perception for more accurate part segmentation.

To achieve accurate part mask completion while pre-

AEM adopts an adaptive iterative enhancement strategy. At
each iteration, it evaluates the enhancement effectiveness
by computing an iteration gain, and introduces a compen-
sation mechanism for small parts with weak initial activa-

termination for small parts that, due to their limited initial

5. Experiments
5.1. Experimental Setup
Datasets.We evaluate HOPS on three part segmentation
datasets: (1) Pascal-Part-116 [47], an optimized version of
Pascal-Part [4], with 8,431 training and 850 test images,
covering 116 part categories across 17 object classes. (2)
ADE20K-Part-234 [47], derived from ADE20K [54], con-
taining 7,347 training and 1,016 validation images, with
234 part categories under 44 object classes. (3) PartIma-
geNet [18] divides 158 object classes from ImageNet [11]
into 11 superclasses with consistent part structures, includ-
ing roughly 16k training and 2.9k validation images.
Evaluation Tasks & Metrics.Following prior works
[7, 8, 47], we evaluate HOPS on two tasks: (1)Zero-shot
part segmentation, assessing generalization to unseen part
categories. Pascal-Part-116 contains 74 seen and 42 unseen
part categories, ADE20K-Part-234 includes 176 seen and
58 unseen part categories, and PartImageNet follows prior
splits [7, 8, 47] with 25 seen and 15 unseen classes among
40 selected object classes. (2)Cross-dataset part segmenta-
tion, evaluating cross-domain generalization. PartImageNet
(OOD) [18] is an out-of-distribution variant of PartIma-
geNet, where the training, validation, and test categories

6. Conclusion
HOPS proposes a bidirectional semantic-structural atten-
tion fusion mechanism that harnesses the complementary
strengths of CLIP and DINO. Specifically, the proposed
AFM refines cost maps through semantic-structural atten-
tion to mitigate object over-segmentation, while AEM iter-
atively enhances part activations under the guidance of the
affinity matrix to alleviate part under-segmentation. By in-
corporating semantic-structural attention in both modules,
HOPS achieves precise and structure-aware part segmenta-
tion in open-vocabulary scenarios.
