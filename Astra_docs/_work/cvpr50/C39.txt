Abstract
High-resolution remote sensing imagery exhibits complex
spatial regularities where topology, continuity, and region
adjacency govern semantic organization. However, existing
remote sensing image semantic segmentation (RSISS) net-
works, being predominantly discriminative, estimate strong
posteriors from data while lacking generative priors that
encode such structural dependencies. This imbalance leads
to fragmented boundaries, texture overfitting, and poor
cross-dataset generalization. We address this challenge
by reformulating RSISS as posterior inference grounded
in generative structural priors, introducingHySeg, a hy-
brid generative–discriminative segmentation paradigm that
learns structure-consistent priors through generative mod-
eling and guides posterior inference for remote sensing
segmentation. At its core, the MeanStruct module, a
MeanFlow-based generative prior learner, models semantic
topology as a continuous stochastic field, while the Prior-
to-Affinity Projection (P2A) dynamically transforms this
field into topology-aware, class-conditional affinities that
guide posterior inference in the Dynamic Affinity-driven
Segmentation (DAS) head. Our approach is model-agnostic
and seamlessly integrates with diverse backbones, consis-
tently improving structural coherence and generalization.
Across four challenging RSISS benchmarks, HySeg achieves
state-of-the-art performance and advances remote sensing
segmentation from appearance-based perception to struc-
tural reasoning. Our code is available athttps://
github.com/HeryJie/HySeg.

To close this gap, we move beyond appearance-

that they reflect the continuous geography and inter-region

We pursue this question from a generative perspective
and introduce MeanStruct, a MeanFlow-based [12, 14] prior
learner that models structure as a continuous transport pro-
cess in label space. It estimates a mean-transport (average-
velocity) field that maps simple structural prototypes to the
empirical distribution of segmentation maps under supervi-
sion. By modellinghowsemantic topology evolves, rather
than merelywhichlabels appear, MeanStruct learns a prob-
abilistic structural prior that can better capture the intrin-
sic geometry of real-world layouts. While MeanStruct pro-

ogy, the learned prior remains abstract and must be trans-
lated into a form that can guide pixel-level reasoning. To
this end, we propose the Prior-to-Affinity Projection (P2A),
which operationalizes the generative prior by translating
it into topology-aware, class-conditional affinity kernels
that regulate pixel interactions and enforce structural coher-
ence across regions. Acting as a structural translator, P2A
bridges generative reasoning and discriminative inference,
transforming the implicit topology encoded by the prior

knowledge governs semantic organization.

backbones, consistently improving structural coherence and
generalization. Our maincontributionsare threefold:

age semantic segmentation (RSISS) as posterior infer-

ing perception and reasoning through a unified genera-
tive–discriminative paradigm.
•Hybrid Approach.We proposeHySeg, a novel hybrid
generative–discriminative framework that unifies gener-

2. Related Work
Remote Sensing Image Semantic Segmentation.RSISS
is a core task in geospatial vision that converts aerial or
satellite imagery into dense land-cover maps [43]. CNN-
based models such as DeepLab [4, 5, 58], HRNet [54],
PSPNet [63], EFRNet [23], and EKENet [33] leverage
atrous convolution, feature reconstruction, and knowl-
edge enhancement to capture multi-scale context, form-
ing the foundation of modern high-resolution semantic
segmentation pipelines. Transformer-based designs (e.g.,
SegFormer [60], Swin-Unet [2], RSSFormer [61], scale-
adaptive models [29], and RS-vHeat [16]) further improve
global context modeling and long-range reasoning, achiev-
ing state-of-the-art results on diverse benchmarks. Be-
yond closed-set settings, recent vision–language models
such as RMSIN [26], SegEarth-OV [21], and related formu-
lations [18, 48, 62] enable category-agnostic understanding.
Complementary research in domain adaptation and general-
ization [7, 27, 52] mitigates cross-sensor and regional shifts,
while self- and weakly supervised methods [1, 19, 66] re-
duce annotation demands by exploiting structural cues in
unlabeled data. Generative paradigms, including diffusion-
based methods [42, 65, 68], further advance RSISS by
learning data distributions and representation alignment. In
contrast, our approach unifies generative and discriminative
paradigms within a single inference framework that learns
structural priors through generation and refines them via
discrimination.

erative transport modeling views data generation as learn-
ing continuous velocity fields that move samples from a
prior to the data manifold, offering a physically inter-
pretable alternative to stochastic diffusion. Flow Match-

3. Method
Existing RSISS models are mainly discriminative, focus-
ing on appearance cues while ignoring structural depen-
dencies. We instead formulate segmentation as posterior
inference constrained by generative structural priors. Our
approach couples a generative prior learner with a dis-
criminative segmentor guided by learned dynamic affini-
ties (Fig. 2). Specifically, Stage I (MeanStruct) mod-
els semantic topology as a continuous flow field, learning
structure-consistent priors through a one-step MeanFlow
update. Stage II translates the learned priors into topology-
aware affinities throughP2A, which regulate prior-guided
message passing inDAS. The overall design is backbone-
agnostic and generalizes well across architectures.

ity and adjacency encoded in the prior. In this formulation,
DAS realizes posterior inference regularized by the gener-
ative prior fieldf e, aligning discriminative decoding with
structural consistency.

4. Experiments
4.1. Experimental Setup
Datasets and Evaluation Metrics.We evaluate on four
benchmarks: LoveDA [55], UA Vid [38], ISPRS Potsdam,
and ISPRS Vaihingen [44]. LoveDA, with a ground sam-
pling distance (GSD) of 30 cm, covers seven land-cover
classes from urban and rural scenes. UA Vid provides 420
high-resolution UA V images (3840×2160or4096×2160)
across eight urban categories, following the splits in [57].
Vaihingen (9 cm GSD) and Potsdam (5 cm GSD) each con-
tain six classes, following the splits in Wang et al. [57].
We adopt standard metrics including overall accuracy (OA),
mean intersection-over-union (mIoU), and mean F1-score
(mF1) to measure pixel accuracy, class-wise balance, and
region overlap [8, 9, 32].
Implementation Details.To validate the generality of
HySeg, we integrate it into multiple state-of-the-art
segmentation frameworks, including UNetFormer [57]
(ResNet18/34/50 [15]), LSKNet [24] (LSKNet T/S), DC-
Swin [56] (Swin T/B/S [30]), and D2LS [69] (Con-
vNeXt B [31]). This setup ensures coverage of both
CNN- and Transformer-based paradigms, from lightweight
to large-scale backbones. All experiments are implemented
in PyTorch and trained on a single NVIDIA A800 GPU
using AdamW. We follow the original training settings of
each backbone [24, 57] for fair comparison across datasets
(LoveDA, UA Vid, Potsdam, Vaihingen). During inference,
multi-scale testing and horizontal flipping are applied to en-
hance robustness.

4.3. Ablation Study
We conduct ablation experiments on the LoveDA dataset
to quantify the contribution of each HySeg component.
Four representative backbones are evaluated—UNetFormer
(ResNet18/50), LSKNet (LSKNet S), DCSwin (Swin B),
and D2LS(ConvNeXt B). Results are reported as mIoU (↑)
on the LoveDAonline testingserver under identical training
settings as the baselines, ensuring that the observed gains
originate solely from HySeg’s architecture.
Component-Wise Analysis.As summarized in Tab. 3,
the ablation on LoveDA reveals how HySeg components
complement each other across five backbones (ResNet-
18/50, Swin B, LSKNet S, ConvNeXt B). The baseline
performance gradually improves as additional modules are
introduced. While DAS alone brings only modest gains,
fusing MeanStruct priors already enhances mIoU, show-
ing that generative cues are beneficial even without ex-
plicit affinity modeling. Once P2A is enabled, priors are
properly translated into class- and topology-aware affinities,
leading to a substantial leap. Disabling affinity propaga-
tion (MeanStruct + P2A + DAS w/o Affinity & Message)
causes a pronounced decline, highlighting the importance
of message passing for structural reasoning. Removing self-
attention leads to only mild degradation, implying that local
propagation guided by priors dominates the learning pro-
cess. The complete HySeg consistently yields the strongest
results, underscoring the effectiveness of its unified genera-
tive–discriminative design.

enhancing structural coherence and segmentation accuracy.

5. Conclusions
We introduced HySeg, a hybrid generative–discriminative
framework that reformulates remote sensing segmentation
as posterior inference guided by generative structural pri-
ors. By coupling the MeanStruct prior learner with the P2A
and DAS modules, HySeg bridges appearance perception
and structural reasoning. This design enables topology-
aware priors learned via generative transport to guide dis-
criminative inference, yielding spatially coherent and se-
mantically consistent predictions. Extensive experiments
on four benchmarks demonstrate its strong accuracy, robust-
ness, and efficiency. Overall, HySeg represents a promising
step toward unifying generative structure learning with dis-
criminative visual understanding.
