Abstract
Open-vocabulary semantic segmentation aims to en-
able models to segment arbitrary categories. Currently,
though pre-trained Vision-Language Models (VLMs) like
CLIP have established a robust foundation for this task
by learning to match text and image representations from
large-scale data, their lack of pixel-level recognition neces-
sitates further ﬁne-tuning. Most existing methods leverage
text as a guide to achieve pixel-level recognition. How-
ever , the inherent biases in text semantic descriptions and
the lack of pixel-level supervisory information make it chal-
lenging to ﬁne-tune CLIP-based models effectively. This pa-
per considers leveraging image-text data to simultaneously
capture the semantic information contained in both image
and text, thereby constructing Dual Semantic Guidance and
corresponding pixel-level pseudo annotations. Particularly,
the visual semantic guidance is enhanced via explicitly ex-
ploring foreground regions and minimizing the inﬂuence of
background. The dual semantic guidance is then jointly uti-
lized to ﬁne-tune CLIP-based segmentation models, achiev-
ing decent ﬁne-grained recognition capabilities. As the
comprehensive evaluation shows, our method outperforms
state-of-art results with large margins, on eight commonly
used datasets with/without background.
1. Introduction
Open-V ocabulary Semantic Segmentation (OVSS) aims to
segment arbitrary categories in open environments, distin-
guishing itself from traditional semantic segmentation [ 1–
3], which operates within a predeﬁned set of categories. Re-
cently, Visual-Language Models (VLMs) such as CLIP [ 4]
and Align [5] demonstrate exceptional capabilities in align-
ing textual descriptions with corresponding images, mak-
ing them highly suitable for open-vocabulary tasks. How-
ever, these VLMs with image-level visual-text alignment
lack ﬁne-grained expressive power, resulting in poor seg-
mentation precision when directly applied to the OVSS

task. Currently, several approaches [ 6– 10] train segmen-
tation models based on VLMs using image-text data which
is rich in semantic information. This enables the models
to acquire ﬁne-grained segmentation capabilities while re-
taining their generalization ability across open-vocabulary
categories, which provides a promising strategy for tackling
semantic segmentation tasks in open-vocabulary scenarios.
Let’s look deeper in open-vocabulary semantic segmen-
tation tasks utilizing VLMs: the core principle involves
aligning pixel-level features with text embeddings, and as-
signing each pixel a category based on these alignments.
Currently, some methods [ 6, 8] trained on image-text data
rely on the VLM model’s ability to align part of images
with the complete texts, establishing weak constraints for
segmentation training. In contrast to employing complete
texts, some methods [ 7, 9] attempt to extract nouns from
texts to obtain category semantic information that matches
local regions in images. However, nouns lacking detailed
descriptions can suffer from “Inherent biases”, which arise
from the semantic disparity between coarse-grained text and
corresponding images. In image-text data, the nouns in text
often offers coarse-grained information, where ”cat” and
”dog” are both labeled as ”animal” (Fig 1.a), yet there are
substantial differences in image level. Moreover, some ex-
isting methods [ 6, 9] lack the pixel-level semantic super-
visory information to ﬁne-tune VLMs. As a result, poorly
segmented images can still align with the corresponding text
well, as shown in Fig 1.b.
In this paper, we denote the semantic information used
for matching images pixels as semantic guidance .W e
identify that the crucial factor in improving the model’s
ﬁne-grained segmentation performance is the establishment
of granular semantic guidance (at both text and image level)
and accurate segmentation annotations. To acquire more
precise semantic guidance and segmentation labels, we ex-
plore the feasibility of extracting semantic guidance from
images which are abundant in semantic content. The most
direct form is the various instances within an image, as these
instances provide an unbiased semantic representation of
the corresponding regions. By utilizing foundation models
such as SAM [11], we can efﬁciently extract a large amount

Speciﬁcally, we propose Dual Semantic Guidance to ex-
tract semantic information from both visual and textual per-
spectives within image-text data. The pipeline consists of
Dual Semantic Guidance Generation (DSGG) and Dual Se-
mantic Guidance Segmentation (DSGS). In DSGG, we uti-
lize SAM to extract instances from images, and enhance vi-
sual semantics to focus more on instance through a training-
free Region Awareness Attention module. Subsequently,
through clustering and ﬁltering, we attain high-quality, in-
dependent visual semantic guidance. On the other hand,
independent nouns are extracted from the text, which are
then matched with the visual semantic guidance to construct
textual semantic guidance. In DSGS, we design a training
strategy for dual semantic guidance to collaboratively en-
hance the model’s ﬁne-grained representation capabilities.
Additionally, we develop the Background Screening mod-
ule, which integrates the image feature map with the se-
mantic guidance to jointly predict the image background.
Our method surpasses state-of-the-art results on various test
datasets, including coco-stuff [ 12], ADE [ 13], etc., achiev-
ing an average mIoU of 48.3 on three datasets with back-
ground, and 42.8 on ﬁve datasets without background.
2. Related Work
2.1. Vision Foundation Models
The latest advancements in foundational vision models
demonstrate that large-scale pre-training techniques have
facilitated the development of potent and versatile mod-
els [ 5, 11]. These models exhibit remarkable adaptability
across various tasks [ 14– 19]. The emergence of highly ex-
pressive vision-language models, such as Align and CLIP ,
has been witnessed in the realm of large-scale contrastive
pre-training of text-image pairs. Furthermore, SAM [ 11]
has undergone iterative training on billions of weakly an-
notated images, achieving remarkable segmentation per-
formance, but it lacks semantic discriminative capabilities.
Researchers have proposed various integration strategies:
SAM-CLIP [ 20] constructs a uniﬁed model that skillfully
integrates the functionalities of SAM and CLIP into a single
framework. Open-V ocabulary SAM [21] integrates a CLIP
encoder with a SAM decoder, bridging these two distinct
components through knowledge distillation. Additionally,
some methods [ 22– 24] integrate multiple models [ 25– 27]
to collaboratively form a powerful open-vocabulary seg-
menter. However, such methods often demand substantial
computational resources.
Compared to these workﬂow-based integrated models, our
approach relies solely on CLIP’s image encoder as the seg-
menter during the testing phase, achieving open-vocabulary
segmentation without adding any additional modules.
2.2. Open-V ocabulary Semantic Segmentation
The emergence of Vision-Language Models (VLMs) with
aligned image-language representations has brought new
perspectives to open-vocabulary tasks. However, these
VLM models prioritize the alignment between the holis-
tic image and its textual description, overlooking the ﬁne-
grained discriminative capabilities of individual pixels.
Some works [ 28– 30] employ supplementary modules and
mechanisms to enhance the VLM’s segmentation capabil-
ities, which entail substantial annotation costs. On the
other hand, some studies have focused on developing open-
vocabulary segmentation capabilities from weakly labeled
or even unlabeled data, categorized into text-supervised se-
mantic segmentation and additional model-guided semantic

The text-supervised methods primarily focus on using
text to establish semantic segmentation objectives, lever-
aging image-text matching to supervise model training:
GroupViT [ 7] matches text with clustered image regions.
SegCLIP [ 31] augments CLIP with an inserted grouping

matching as a supervisory signal; CoDe [ 9] decomposes
text into noun phrases, focusing on matching image re-

tional models to provide segmentation pseudo-labels, CLIP-
DINOiser [ 32] utilizes the DINO [ 27] to generate coarse
masks, enforcing semantic consistency within the masks;

tation results and employs learnable text features as guid-
ance to supervise model training.
Most of the aforementioned methods rely on text while
overlooking the potential of image instances as high-quality
visual semantic guidance to align model outputs. In case of
lacking pixel-level annotations, their performances are sub-
optimal. Our approach focuses on constructing dual seman-
tic guidance from both image and text, generating pixel-
level pseudo-labels to jointly facilitate the adaptation of the
CLIP model to dense visual prediction tasks.

4.2. Implementation Detail
We generate a vast number of masks via SAM ViT-
B, and utilize the part-of-speech tagging algorithm from
NLTK [33] for noun selection. For K-Means [ 47] cluster-
ing, we set k = 5. For the segmentation model, we employ
MaskCLIP [48] with ViT-B/16. For Region Awareness At-
tention, we utilize CLIP with ViT-B/16. During the exper-
iments, we set total batchsize = 128 with a initial learning
rate = 6.4e-4, and 30,000 iterations are conducted (we open
the last 6 layers of image encoder for training). Further-
more, we ultimately train the model for additional 1,000
iterations on textual semantic guidance alone to adapt it to
the textual modality received during inference. The cosine
schedule [49] is adopted to adjust the learning rate, and the
weight decay of the AdamW optimizer [ 50] is set to 0.05.
The model is trained on two RTX 3090 GPUs for ﬁve hours.
In addition, we solely use image encoder for the datasets
without background (w/o bg); whereas for datasets with
background (w/ bg), we additionally activate the Back-
ground Screening for background prediction.

4.5. Discussion
Alternative Ways for Region Awareness Attention In
Table 5, we evaluated ﬁve substitution methods to validate
the efﬁcacy of Region Awareness Attention. Firstly,we in-
troduced Gaussian noise(see “Gaussian”) to the background
regions, which resulted in a decline in performance, be-
cause the addition of Gaussian noise does not ensure that
the model focuses on the foreground regions. We also at-
tempt to mask the attention of the patch tokens correspond-
ing to the background regions(see “mask background”), and
ﬁnd that this strategy severely impaired the model’s feature
encoding capabilities. Concurrently, we explore learnable
strategy: replacing background tokens with class-speciﬁc
tokens [ 53] (see “class prompt”). This strategy is inferior
to those of our method, as the lack of training stage pre-
vent these training based methods from effectively adapt-
ing. Finally, we refer to the mask pooling strategy of
MaskCLIP(see “mask pooling”), whose experimental re-
sults are lower than ours, as the low-resolution feature map
of CLIP will contain background information and interfere
with the instance feature expression.

performed poorly under this loss, as it neglects the infor-

the Feature-level TCL loss [ 6] (see “TCL loss”), which fa-
cilitates the efﬁcient computation of features for negative
instances. We ﬁnd that the model’s performance is infe-
rior to ours, as this loss function compares whole-image
features rather than instance features with the semantic
guidance to compute the loss. Additionally, we replace
the average pooling with similarity-weighted pooling (see
“Ours(Global)”), but this result in a decline. Because this
method places greater emphasis on pixel features that are
closer to the semantic guidance, thereby underestimating
the importance of hard positive pixels for training.
Limitation In the Dual Semantic Guidance Generation,
we rely on SAM to generate a multitude of masks for each
image, a process that is time-consuming. Furthermore, akin
to existing methods in OVSS, our visualization results still
suffer from rough boundaries. To boost computational efﬁ-
ciency, we opt for K=5 in K-Means due to its superior per-
formance. Adaptive selection of K is intriguing, which will
be considered in future work.
5. Conclusion
Pre-trained Vision-Language Models (VLMs) establish the
foundation for recognizing open-vocabulary categories in
semantic segmentation tasks. Nonetheless, the dearth of
pixel-level annotations in image-text data hinders the provi-
sion of thorough and accurate supervisory signals for VLM
ﬁne-tuning. This paper introduces Semantic Guidance Gen-
eration, which leverages extensive image-text data from vi-
sual and textual dimensions, offering comprehensive and
precise data to enable VLMs to attain pixel-level segmen-
tation capability. Furthermore, we develop the Dual Se-
mantic Guidance to facilitate efﬁcient model training. Our
methodology concurrently and effectively elevates segmen-
tation performance in open-vocabulary scenarios, both with
and without the background category.
