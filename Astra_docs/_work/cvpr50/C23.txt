Abstract
Weakly supervised semantic segmentation (WSSS) has gar-
nered considerable attention due to its effective reduction of
annotation costs. Most approaches utilize Class Activation
Maps (CAM) to produce pseudo-labels, thereby localizing
target regions using only image-level annotations. How-
ever, the prevalent methods relying on vision transform-
ers (ViT) encounter an “over-expansion” issue, i.e., CAM
incorrectly expands high activation value from the target
object to the background regions, as it is difficult to learn
pixel-level local intrinsic inductive bias in ViT from weak
supervisions. To solve this problem, we propose a Pro-
gressive Confidence Region Expansion (PCRE) framework
for WSSS, it gradually learns a faithful mask over the tar-
get region and utilizes this mask to correct the confusion in
CAM. PCRE has two key components: Confidence Region
Mask Expansion (CRME) and Class-Prototype Enhance-
ment (CPE). CRME progressively expands the mask in the
small region with the highest confidence, eventually encom-
passing the entire target, thereby avoiding unintended cov-
erage of background areas. CPE aims to enhance mask gen-
eration in CRME by leveraging the similarity between the
learned, dataset-level class prototypes and patch features
as supervision to optimize the mask output from CRME. Ex-
tensive experiments demonstrate that our method outper-
forms the existing single-stage and multi-stage approaches
on the PASCAL VOC and MS COCO benchmark. Our code
is available at https://github.com/xxf011/WSSS-PCRE.

cent years. These weak annotations include image-level

boxes [6, 7]. Among them, image-level annotation is one of

Despite these advancements, a significant challenge re-
mains that these methods tend to incorrectly expand CAM
from the target object region to the background with high
activation values. We call this phenomenon as over-
expansion of CAM. As shown in Fig. 1 (a), the target bird
region easily spreads to the adjacent tree trunk ( i.e., back-
ground) in the generated CAM after training. This is due
to that the weak annotations fail to learn inductive biases in
modeling local feature structures, which is lacking in ViT,
and high feature similarities between the target and back-
ground result in unreliable pseudo labels of background. In
Fig. 1 (b), as training proceeds, the high feature similarity
expands from the target inferior to its exterior background,
further intensifying the over-expansion in CAM.
To solve the above issue, we propose a novel weakly
supervised semantic segmentation framework via Progres-
sive Confidence Region Expansion (PCRE), which gradu-
ally generates an accuracy and complete target mask for ef-
fective WSSS. As illustrated in Fig. 2, the proposed PCRE
consists of two modules, Confidence Region Mask Ex-
pansion (CRME) and Class-Prototype Enhancement (CPE).
Specifically, CRME incrementally expands the scope of the
segmentation mask in small steps to constrain the mask to
diffuse background areas. It begins by selecting a mask
over the region with the highest confidence. After that, it
restricts the mask generation range at each expansion step
to progressively incorporate additional masks based on their
patch confidence levels, which produces an accurate refined
mask over the entire target. However, relying solely on the
CRME mask expansion strategy still struggles to fully cap-
ture the semantic precision of target regions. To address
this, we propose CPE to further guide the CRME expansion
process. We observe that shallow layers in the early stages
can capture discriminative local features relevant to the tar-
get, but these features tend to be overly smoothed under the
global attention mechanism as training progresses. As such,
CPE introduces the EMA to preserve these early-stage dis-
criminative features, which optimizes dense similarity maps
through class prototype alignment to capture fine-grained
semantic correlations across images.
Our main contributions can be summarized as:
• We propose Progressive Confidence Region Expansion
(PCRE) to address the over-expansion issue for effective
WSSS. By progressively expanding high-confidence re-
gions, PCRE significantly improves CAM pseudo labels
and segmentation performance.
• We design a CRME module that incrementally expands
the mask’s scope in small steps, effectively avoiding un-
intended background coverage. Additionally, we develop
a CPE module to learn rich, dataset-level representations
for each class, generating the prototypical CAM for ef-
fective supervision.
• Experimental results demonstrate that the superior perfor-
mance of our method. For example, on the COCO 2014
benchmark dataset [17], the proposed PCRE outperforms
SOTA single-stage DIAL [14] and multi-stage PCSS [18]
by 2.8% mIoU and 1.5%, respectively.
2. Related work
Weakly-Supervised Semantic Segmentation. Traditional
WSSS [2, 8] primarily relies on CNN to localize target re-
gions through CAM [11]. However, CAM typically only
covers the most distinctive parts of the objects [19, 20]. To
address this limitation, researchers have proposed methods
such as adversarial erasing [21–23], auxiliary tasks [24–
26], and CRF [27] to expand activation regions and im-
prove the quality of pseudo labels. With the emergence
of ViT [15], researchers have explored the application of
ViT in WSSS, leveraging the global semantic aggregation
capability of the class token to enhance the model’s com-
prehensive focus on target regions. For example, the works
in [28, 29] introduce multiple class-specific tokens and uti-
lized cross-attention maps between class tokens and patch
tokens to acquire positional cues. the works in [12, 30] con-
trast class tokens from full-image labels with those from
cropped uncertain regions. DIAL [14] incorporates cross-
modal contrastive learning to maximize similarity between
class tokens and target text embeddings. CTI [31] enhances
the consistency and class specificity of CAM by injecting
class tokens across different augmentations of the same im-
age and between different images. However, these meth-
ods either employ a single class token for the entire image,
limiting effective class differentiation in multi-class scenar-
ios, or introduce multiple class tokens with cross-attention
mechanisms that introduce noise due to their indirect as-
sociation with the optimization objective. We propose the
CPE module to extract both nuanced semantic information
and representative, dataset-level prototypes for each class.
Progressive Expansion. In the computer vision liter-
ature, the concept of progressive expansion has been ex-
plored in various tasks, but with motivations and imple-
mentations very different from our approach. For instance,
PDEN [32] progressively expands from single to multi-
ple domains to simulate various photometric and geometric
transforms in unseen domains. PSS [33] progressively en-
larges the training set to enhance model resilience against
out-of-distribution classes. BungeeNeRF [34] incremen-
tally expands the training dataset, enabling the model to
robustly capture radiance representations across varying
scales within a scene. ToE [35] progressively expands to-

3. Methods
3.1. Preliminaries
We define X as an input image and y as its correspond-
ing class label, where y ∈ { 0, 1}C indicates the one-hot
vector with the total C classes. In recent WSSS meth-
ods [13, 14], initial pseudo-labels are usually generated
from the features extracted from ViT by CAM [11]. specifi-
cally, the image feature mapF ∈ Rhw×d is extracted by the
backbone, where h, w, and d represent the height, width,
and dimension of the feature map, respectively. Note that
hw also presents the total token number in ViT. Subse-
quently, the image feature F is multiplied by the weights
W cls ∈ RC×d of the classification layer to generate initial
CAMinit ∈ RC×hw, which is formulated as:
CAMinit = Norm(ReLU(W clsF ⊤)), (1)
where Norm(·) denotes the min-max normalization by scal-
ing CAM to the range of [0, 1]. Generally, the pseudo labels
are generated by a threshold β to differentiate between the
foreground and background regions.
3.2. Overview
As presented in Fig. 2, the proposed PCRE aims to re-
duce the over-expansion problem, which comprises two key
components: Confidence Region Mask Expansion (CRME)
and Class-Prototype Enhancement (CPE). CRME dynami-
cally generates masks from small high-confidence regions,
which progressively expand the refined masks mc
r over the
entire target region. CPE employs exponential moving aver-
age (EMA) to dynamically optimize class tokens Z cls, in-
tegrating features from different stages to generate repre-
sentative class prototypes p. The prototypical CAM pro be-
tween class prototypes p and image features F serves as
the supervision for the refined masksmc
r in CRME. Finally,
the pseudo labels are obtained by combining the prototyp-
ical CAM pro and the initial CAM init, which generates the
pseudo-labels to supervise the final segmentation branch.
3.3. Confidence Region Mask Expansion
The core idea of CRME is to gradually expand the mask’s
scope in small increments based on the confidence level of
each patch, to effectively restrict the mask’s spread into the
background regions.

3.4. Class-Prototype Enhancement
CPE aims to learn discriminative, dataset-level prototypes
for each class, which is further utilized in the patch features
to generate high-quality pseudo labels, serving as supervi-
sion for mask generation in CRME.

aims to provide each CLS token with more distinct seman-
tic information by supervising the alignment between CLS
tokens and image features. In the vanilla ViT, the CLS to-

a linear layer and trained using cross-entropy loss with la-
bel y. It enables efficient aggregation of global features for
classification but fails to obtain rich semantic information

further form semantic matches between the CLS tokens and

Datasets and Evaluation Metrics. We conduct experi-
ments on the PASCAL VOC 2012 [40] and MS COCO

(mIoU) metric to assess the experimental results.

5. Conclusion
In this paper, we propose a Progressive Confidence Re-
gion Expansion (PCRE) framework to address the over-
expansion issue in CAM for weakly supervised semantic
segmentation tasks. PCRE allows the gradual generaliza-
tion of a faithful mask covering the entire target region and
introduces representative, dataset-level prototypes for each
class, which rectify excessive expansion to similar back-
ground areas in CAM. Extensive experiments show that
PCRE achieves new state-of-the-art performances.
